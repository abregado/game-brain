BS - Mechanics Suited for single player Exploration

**Time Limit**  
Imposing a limit both the time allowed for each player choice as well as the total number of choices that can be made in one game session.

**Investing in the unknown (more risk=more reward)**  
The player is exploring, and should not know what the outcomes will be before making his choices. Clues and hints are a different matter.

**Clues (breadcrumbs to follow)**  
The player may or may not require a trail to follow. Having many options, some effort should be made to lead the player through the game. At the very least the tutorial or early game sessions should have such a feature.

**Real choices**  
The content being generated an presented as a prize to the player is far from unique. Any content in a generated system could theoretically be generated by any other play session. Thus the player needs to feel like his choices brought him these rewards, not that he is following a predetermined path.

**Jackpot rewards (Every choice is NOT a winner)  
**The rewards given should be constant but serious Rewards should only be found rarely. The drive for the player to find the next "jackpot" should be enough to keep him playing. These Jackpots need to be well designed and large.

**Moderate pacing (Keep the man walking)  
**Exploration players will want to be able to take breaks and plan their own pace. However every effort should be taken to stop a player from slowing down to the point of boredem, even against their will.

**Reactive AI (Equal and opposite)  
**Any time the system does something, it should be BECAUSE OF a choice the player made. Invariably players learn how the AI operates. Once this is known the player simply plays in a way which nullifies the AI and then ignores it. However if the player knows how the AI will react to a choice he is about to make, then that player must think about his reaction to the AI's reaction. This creates strategy on a very basic level (forcing the player to think 3 moves ahead).

**Excess information (Even useless, randomised information)  
**When delivering a rewarding load of content to the player, additional non-important "fluff" can be included as well. This information has no impact on the players choices but is a good way to deliver narrative or to make the reward seem greater than it actually is. Many players find this type of information incredibly rewarding (considering it worldbuilding)

**Unreachable, long term goals (Dream of Infinite Content)  
**From the outset the player should be presented with a few goals that, are reachable given enough time and luck, are effectivly unreachable in a perfect form. The player can work towards these goals and will hopefully get caught up in the meaty bits of your fine game on the way, creating for himself much more exciting goals along the way.

**Decoys (Ball under the cup)  
**No so much false choices, but instead the opportunity for something to be fake. The risk of rare negative impacts should make players think twice before selecting the "easily best" options in any given situation. Players should think "what will i get if i make choice A" as well as "what would i do if choice A is a lie"

Decoys should come in both random and detectable trap varieties. After falling for a decoy the player should always be advised how to avoid them in the future.

**Outside, overarching perspective (God view)  
**A great perspective if you want the player to be constantly reminded of how far he has come. Connection to reality suffers of course, and the player cannot easily become Lost in your game world. If the small details of your world are lacking, this is a great way to make the player feel like it is still detailed.

**Narrow personal perspective (Tunnel Vision)  
**Perfect if you want the player to become lost in your world. Opposite to above, the player also should experience a closer connection to self but can only see the world closest to himself. If your world is highly detailed this should be a good option.

**Flat Environment with Features (not every stone is important)  
**Never overload the environment with exciting features. The aim of the game is exploration so the player should have to see more common features than rare ones. The game world should reflect this. Features that are common or semi-common should be interesting the first time, and even perhaps fufil some basic gameplay need that the player will require repeatedly

**Limited obvious high quality choices (first in best dressed)  
**In each area the player should be able to identify the most interesting or rewardling locations immediatly. Other opportunities should be available (sometimes better ones than the obvious shown ones) but they must be searched for.

Reusing content and assests (differences can be shown with numbers)  
The player will be expecting to see a lot of similar things between exciting content. As it is expected, the developer/artist shouldnt feel bad for reusing art assests many times.

**Repetative gameplay  
**Similar as above. The player is expecting repeatative gameplay so it is not as important to create a dynamic user experience. The pacing and rewards should be enough to keep the player interested.

**Static environment  
**If the game world is interesting enough it can be entirely static. While the player is exploring, nothing changes at all. If the player comes back to the same location then it will be exactly the same as before. This should keep development time down and keep the player moving outwards to explore new locations as he can always return later to finish exploring an early part.
