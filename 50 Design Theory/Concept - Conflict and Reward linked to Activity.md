Concept – Conflict caused by Activity

Sounds simple enough but most games have conflict that is not related to Activity. Instead the triggers are often arbitrary, time, or narrative progression based.

This type of conflict generation means that players are always going to be challenged as the better/longer they play, the harder the game works to stop them. Obviously getting the balance correct in this case is the tricky part.

Having conflict levels scale exponentially with Activity gives you an unwinable game. The aim of such a game is not to complete the content, but to survive longer than everyone else (Survival genre with high scores). This style of game is popular at the moment but often the conflict is increased smoothly and is not procedural, and as such, runs out after a while.

Having conflict levels scale logarithmically means the game increases in difficulty quickly at the beginning and then more slowly as time goes on. The first part of any conflict is the most exciting, promoting “Instant Action” style gaming.

The designer could control also count Activity cumulatively. This way the difficulty isnt simply about how much Activity the player has put in NOW, but is the total of how much he has put in ALL TOGETHER. This means that players who have already been very active but continue to be as active or more active. This of course could lead to player burn out, so a suitable method of resting the player must be found as well.

Reward levels could also be linked to Activity. Linking Reward and Conflict to Activity at different rates could be used to create “sweet spots” in the game. These are where players get the most progression for the least risk. Players are rewarded more for going at just the right pace.
