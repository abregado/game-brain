Levelling systems

For this discussion, Difficulty is separate from Content level. Higher level does not mean more difficult.

The most common method of progression is xp and character levelling. There are only two variants that I can find:

Unique content for each level

Dynamic content difficulty scaling

We can also decide if the player controls the difficulty or the developer.

|                 | Player chosen Difficulty | Developer chosen Difficulty   |
|-----------------|--------------------------|-------------------------------|
| Unique Content  | Diablo                   | Dark Souls, Incremental games |
| Dynamic Content | Inquisitor: Martyr       |                               |

Unique content is out of the question for any game I would make because it is simply too expensive. Content is used up once as the player is at the right level. This means you need more content all the time.

If they reach that content when they are too weak or powerful then the experience is made worse. Difficulty lowers one, and exacerbates the other. Balancing is done manually, with earlier content having lower difficulty, and later content having higher difficulty.

Dynamic content seems fine until you boil it down and realise that you are not levelling at all. If the challenge scales to the level, then difficulty only changes how many builds are viable. It follows that certain builds are weak, others strong, and the player is simply doing the work of figuring out which ones to use. If the players preferred build/playstyle lies outside what is usable for the given difficulty the player need to be able to change their preference, or the difficulty.

Is that not the case for the developer choosing difficulty as well? I guess yes. The developer can decide the difficulty is high enough that certain options they have given to the player are not actually viable.

So if that is the cost of difficulty, what is the benefit? What comes to mind:

- Force the player to engage in more mechanics (complexity) as the difficulty increases. eg:// The Witchers potion system, cover mechanics and quick reload in Gears of War.

- Raise the bar to entry.

- Give the player more ego reward for completing content.

- Attract the group of players who value higher difficulty.

- Feeling that you might lose, risk takers.

There is a subset of players who value games more when they are difficult, and discount the value of easy games. Reasons for this might be “sense of pride and accomplishment”. A simplification could be that these are competitive players.

There is a subset of players who are the opposite. Are these players not competitive?

Players can have a sense of accomplishment without Difficulty, but the developer does not decide for that to happen. The developer puts mechanics and systems in their game to offer that feeling. It is up to the player to decide if they feel that way.
