Inventory management in games

First question. Does the game need inventory management? If no, then don't have inventory management. That is not to say "don't have inventory". This means, have as few of these as possible:

- Weight limits

- Space limits

- Stack sizes

- Slot/item types (fluids/big small/ECT)

If your game does need inventory management then think about which elements compliment the main mechanics, and make the system as simple (not simple to use) as possible.

Examples:

Space station 13 is a game about complex interactions. Deciding which items to put in your hands, which on your accessible belt and which in your backpack is a key part of the game. Here is gameplay and inventory management is interchangable.

Conan Exiles is a game about melee combat, exploration and taking risks to harvest material far from your fortress. Here the inventory management is too detailed, and detracts from the core gameplay.

Subnautica is an exploration game. The player can only be our for a limited time before returning, and has a very limited space. The inventory management is very simple. Limited inventory slots makes sense for the gameplay, but limited slots in your huge sub or underwater base do not.

Factorio is a game about building factories. It's inventory system allows you to store a large amount of resources, but it forces you to keep many different kinds in your inventory at one time. Since you have so many types, it is easy to run out of space for one more type. This hinders early game enjoyment. Another sacrilege is offering quality of life upgrades as "end game content".

Mechanics that I liked or want to see

Placing a chest next to another one just increases the size of the chest. They are all interacted with as a group.

“Single axis” inventory. Use either Volume, weight or item types, but not more than one of them.

Sorting by item categories. Needs categories that matter.

Workbenches can draw from containers nearby automatically.

Keyboard/controller driven inventories, not drag and drop or mouse driven.

Player only activates interactions, and the items are taken automatically from them or nearby containers.

List of icons top and bottom with a button to move items from top to bottom. Similar to Rimworlds trading gui, but horizonal would work better with icons (and bumper buttons)
