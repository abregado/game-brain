Concept: Iterative Research based crafting

Some current crafting mechanics in games

- Players can craft items with fixed stats at certain points in the game

- Items stats are generated randomly from a list of abilities when crafted

- Players craft items from a list of generic items

- Players craft items with no abilities and then "imbue" them later

Iterative Research mixes some of these concepts together. When a player wishes to craft something they receive a simple schematic. As they craft this item the schematic gets better, but in random bursts. The player can then decide to work on further developing the new iteration, or scrap the changes and try for something else.

Example:

The player wants to craft a new firearm. The game gives firearms the statistics of: Range, Damage and Weight. After purchasing and studying a weapon of his choice, the player gets a schematic of a firearm that is not quite as good as the one he dismantled. By spending resources on it, he can make it better. A considerable amount of resources must be spent, then the player receives a schematic that is better in two statistics and worse in another. Results should only be known after one of the item has been constructed. The player can then decide to keep the schematic the way it is, or revert it to its original state and try again. He may also improve the schematic again to make its product even more powerful.

The more resources spent on the schematic before asking for a change, the more benifits the new schematic will have. There is also more overall randomization, and such, risk that you dont get what you want. A player must choose to improve slowly and surely to get exactly what he wants, or rely on pot luck and get whats given to him.

Other uses:

- Technology trees

- Exploration (presented with a reward, choose to continue further or accept reward)

- Quest rewards
