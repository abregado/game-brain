## Intro - Well known XP systems

Many new ways to deal with player advancement have been hitting the market recently

WoW uses the classic XP point gain, each level having exponentially more XPs needed to reach, but harder enemies giving more XP points when killed.

Some RPGs give XP points only for completing the narrative or quests.

Some have linear XP point requirements

Lately "Skill Rust" has been seen in quite a few games, where the skills a player does not use deteriorate over time.

Ultima Online, one of the first MMOs used a levelless, skill point maximum system. In this system a player could gain new skills and attributes until a certain level. From then on gaining one skill or attribute point would "rust" another less used skill.

EVE Online gives players XP for every moment they have an active subscription and gives players who focus on one attribute an efficency bonus. Gaining every skill in the game world to maximum level would take ~10 years of active account time.

Perpetuum uses a similar system but awards the skill points to a "skill bank". They stay stored up and players can spend them at any time to gain levels instantly. There is also a character reset system so that players can reallocate their skill points.

Experience systems are also great for the New Player Experience or tutorial section of a game. A new player can become overloaded with new information so skills, abilities and new game mechanic concepts are dished out over the first few hours (sometimes minutes) of gameplay.

## 1. Fluctuating specializations 

Combine limited skill points, rusting but reduce the grind to be an effective player, all without throwing new players in the deep end.

New characters begin with their maximum allotment of skills and attributes. Their should be many different categories that effect one another (no role or profession should use just one of these). New players can complete easier content from any role based part of the game, and are equipped to be able to complete the tutorial content without making any changes.

The player can try out all the different types of content available. Different skill groups can be marked as either increasing or decreasing, or stable. Eventually the character will be the best it can be at the roles the player wishes to partake in. At any time the player can retrain for other roles, or can retrain due to their requirements in game changing. The game might even have a primary focus that changes thoughout. Example:// more physical prowess could be useful during the early game, but magical prowess leads to an easier end game.

A limit on the maximum any one XP field could reach during the tutorial content could also stop players making bad decisions early on. Any bad choices a player makes should be able to be undone easily once the error is realised.

The starting skill distribution could be changed to make it easier for newer players. Increasing defensive skills from the outset, or creating sensible builds could lead to less initial frustration.

## 2.Temporary Experience with Hurdles

### **2.1 Skill point version**. 

A system similar to being awarded scout badges. The player has to complete so many grouped tasks within a time limit. At this point he/she is awarded the next level in this area. Failing to meet enough objectives in the time limit means having to start over again.

Skill rust could be used but players cannot lose an entire level. They simply make gaining the next level harder.

### **2.2 Level based version**. 

XP points can be given to the player and if enough are accrued in a 30 day period the player goes up a level. Only one level per 30 days can be awarded but some other reward could be given for players accruing many extra XP. Each day the player loses the XP points that were earnt 30 days ago.

This could work just as well using game time. In a single player RPG a player must rush to finish quests and objectives before the game time limit runs out. Also would make areas with high quest density more useful.

### 2.2.1 Levelling out based on playskill

Instead of the amount of XP going up each level, the time you have to collect it drops. Eventually players will reach a maximum level based on their playtime and will need to seek out certain parts of the content and rebuild parts of their character to get the next level. At this point gaining a level isn’t a given certainty, but is instead something special a player must work for.

## 3 Linear Experience with Upkeep

Instead of making each consecutive level cost more XP, players can build up XP to an XP bank and can spend the point at any time on Skills that go up in a linear fashion. For every level that the character has, a % of future experience is taken away. This will encourage players to only select increases when they feel they must. The content will continue to be challenging and players who chose to keep the content harder will get a bonus. Perhaps at the end of the game the total experience earnt could be kept as a high score. Higher scores means the player succeeded because of player skill more than character skill.

To explain this a little better: Experience goes into XP Bank. Purchasing upgrades is instantaneous. Each upgrade gives you a penalty to future experience gain, but does not affect prices or your XP bank.

I can see this effecting new players negatively, as they will need to spend their experience as they get it to stay alive.

Every upgrade to a character will need to cost experience. Characters should not gain passive bonuses as they level up.

If the maximum skill penalty is 100% then there is effectively a Level Cap. Players will earn the maximum spendable Experience as quick as possible, then spend it all. The design at this point needs an additional reward for saving Experience.

If the maximum skill penalty cannot reach 100% then there is no Level Cap. Players will grind forever until they have every skill. The Level Cap becomes the total number of upgrades in your game.

## 4 Iterative Experience and Leveling

A new character gains experience points as normal (Quests and Monsters). This base level is not enough for the player to advance as fast as the enemies. Once defeated, the character begins anew but with an Experience Gain bonus. The more a player has his characters die, the more powerful he will become. However a mechanic to keep players from needlessly killing their characters repeatedly at the start is needed.

Perhaps the Experience gain can be modified based on how far though the game the dead character has progressed. Dying repeatedly in the early game would only provide a small boost, while dying near the end would give a massive boost, allowing the early game content to be rushed through once more. Then the Experience Gain bonus could be based on the farthest point the player has ventured. For example, if a player reaches the 50% mark, then their new bonus overrides their old bonus from when they reached the 20% mark.

Notoriety (leaderboards) should be given to players who complete the game with as little Experience Gain bonus as possible. Players also need the ability to reset their skill gain.

## 5 Give all EXP at the start of the game

A new player is given every XP point at the start of the game and as the game progresses more options to spend them on become available. In addition existing experience options become cheaper the longer the player waits, promoting keeping your character as powerless as possible while still progressing.

The player will get more total power for the endgame (bosses, ect) by waiting as long as possible.

Difficulty could be adjusted by reducing the amount of experience given at the start, or by reducing the rate at which skill options cheapen (this might just result in players spending as much as possible at the start).

Some enemies should be far easier to dispatch with particular skills, prompting the player to spend some XP at that time, or attempt the fight without bonuses.

New players may spend too much at the beginning of the game and not be powerful enough during the late game. A recommendation of how much to spend at each stage could help this, or simply.

### 5.1 Amendment

In an open world game, where the player can access all areas from the start, this experience system could work well. It should be very easy to develop a focused character to defeat one area, but incredibly hard to develop a character that can beat all areas. Then make defeating all areas with one character the goal. Add permadeath on there and VIOLA! RPG with strategic levelling.

## 6 Experience is the ability to modify your skills before battle

Instead of players becoming more powerful, they instead earn the ability to modify their skills and abilities before a fight. As seen in Arkham Horror (Focus points).

New players spend the tutorial gaining points in skills as per a typical RPG. By the end they are maxed out entirely. Then as they fight their way through the game they earn XP. When they have enough they go up a "Focus Level". At the start of each fight a player can modify his skills/attributes layout based on his Focus Level. The fight begins and if he succeds then he earns XP and moves onto the next part of the game.

Normally when something atatcks the character, the player can see a breakdown of what enemies will be faced BEFORE changing his layout. If something ambushes the character then the layout choices must be made without seeing the opponents (promoting a more balanced playstyle when ambushed). Being prepared for a fight will make it a lot easier.

## Ever increasing XP

Give players xp at an ever decreasing rate. The players must choose which skill to work towards and those points are allocated the that skill. When the skill is full, it is unlocked.

This is similar to EVE skill training, but with the rate ever decreasing, players know they can’t have every skill.

The mechanic of choosing a skill before you get it means that the game plays more on the consequence of a choice, rather than the choice itself. Players need to think ahead to their “big game plan” rather than just picking something on a whim to get immediately at each level.

This may work well with any game where the players have some rough idea of what they will be facing in the long term. If the endgame is deliberately obfuscated then the players may feel cheated or that they wasted experience points.

## Closing Doors

Normal experience system, but as the player makes choices, others become closed to this character.

Closed doors could be shown to the player before making the choice. EH// choosing A will forbid B.

OR

Closed doors could be randomized, to guide the player to choosing his/her highest priorities first

#### 8.1 Tradtional Closing Doors

display choices and concequences as junctions in a tree diagram. Inform the player that the option they do not pick will be forever unavailable

#### 8.2 Randomised Field loss

display choices as a pool of options. each time a choice can be made there should be many options, but inform the player one of the options will be removed from the pool permanently, and that the player must select from this pool several more times before getting a new batch.

## Easy Mode after death

Experience as normal, Quests and Monsters give points, gain stats when you level up.

If the player dies many times in a row prompt them to activate "Easy Mode" which will give them another level immediately, but ask them to repay it double over time.

This may result in a feedback loop where the player depends on this system as they can never catch up. However, if the challenges are possible to overcome with high enough Player Skill, then eventually the player will learn and break out of the spiral.
