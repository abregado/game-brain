Questions

Why is this game so good?

- Roman:

  - Lots of hand-made content, more than a modern game normally has

  - Difficulty is well tuned. Getting it on the third try is a sweet spot

- Dave:

  - Team strategy matters.

  - Sticking together and focusing on each other's position is important.

- Peter:

  - Easy to learn. Why does this game even have a tutorial?!

  - Decently hard to master.

  - Unlocks are interesting rather than just being tiered.

  - Different levels mean that different loadouts are worthwhile

- Tom:

  - Chaotic, coop action is inherently fun

  - Core interactions are well done, well polished

  - No “online bullshit”

- Hill:

  - Get straight into the action

  - Straightforward mechanics

  - Hordes feel fun

  - Player skill mechanics (fast reload)

- Ben

  - Shit can get real bad real fast, but thats okay

  - Playing bad is not punished too much

  - Fast rounds & repeat

Where is the game lacking?

- Roman

  - No overview of what players are doing during a round

  - Player models are too similar

- Peter

  - Difficulty increase between levels inside a campaign is off.

- Dave

  - Too simple

  - Gets old too quickly

  - How much ammo is needed is not very easy to discern

  - Levels needing certain classes seems off

  - Objectives were not clear enough

- Tom

  - Engineer is under utilised. Mechanics should be required or not in the game.

  - Some very “Techie” things seemed to be available to multiple classes.

  - Not many closing doors

  - Weak signalling when infinite waves are spawning

- Hillenth

  - No sense of progression

  - Limited “character skills”

  - Character is not often surprised

  - Lacking customization

  - Map interactions could have been more important

- Peter

  - More enemy types are needed

  - Heavy and Officer classes don’t really have a strong niche

  - Visual polish

- Ben

  - Class system is weak

Which mechanics are supporting multiplayer communication?

- Difficulty being tweaked for 4 players (or however many are in)

- Difficulty scaling based on player count

- Player Positioning

- Calling out enemies

- Player loadout and deciding together

- Map layout

- Need to stick together

- Stationary Healing/Recharge mechanics

- Not everyone can do everything. Being alone means death.

- Everyone wants medic all the time

- Friendly fire

- Wave structure supporting choosing when to stop

- Pre-mission strategy chats separate this from the “shorter communication” during the mission. Choose the play and try to do it.

- Facehuggers

- Low player time-to-kill

Is the class system doing its job?

- What is its job?

  - Lets new players know that medic is important

  - Encourage teamplay opportunities, although it does not do this currently

  - Offload responsibility to players that want it

  - Adding identity to the player. Be something special

  - Making it easier to play WITHOUT communication, for example, when playing with randoms.

- Opinion: Do more with it!

  - Tom, Dave,

- Opinion: Get rid of it!

  - Ben, Hill

What does not fit in the design?

- Tom:

  - Ammo packs in the level

What fits well in the design?

- Coop planning features

New Ideas

- Levels where the team needs to split up, each with a Tech

- Respawn points on the map where everyone can be revived

- Tech does damage to friendlies while hacking

- Another player with a shield that needs to stay close to the tech while hacking

- A downed system. Players need to go back to get you.

- Self-revive minigame

- Characters can carry downed people until they respawn

- Longer levels, to really push survival aspects with ammo saving/resource gameplay.

- Spectators can affect the game

What determines if a game is a top down tactical shooter or a “diablo like” hack and slash game?

- Can you attack everything at once?

- Can you deal damage similarly to all enemy types at once?

- Diablo games tend to have “Infinite ammo”

  - Tactical shooter “Should I shoot this enemy?

  - Diablo game you don’t choose, the answer is always yes.

- Story is normally more involved in a Diablo like

What do those games do differently to achieve/not achieve the same goals?

- Helldivers: Ammo drops kill you

- FORCED: Missions have puzzles that encourage communications

- Nine-Parchments: Positioned geographical Healing effects

- Anvil: “Downed” system
