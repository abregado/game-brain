Vermintide

What was the most unique mechanic in each game, or the mechanic that made the biggest difference?

- Planetside 2

  - The size of the battlefield

  - Playercount

  - Mobile spawn, choose where your squad spawns

- Splitgate

  - Gates

  - Game modes

  - Super short round times

- Killing Floor

  - Backtracking

  - Pacing

- Team Fortress 2: MvM

  - Look/Style

  - Personality

- Vermintide

  - Lots of options

  - Good situations where you have to communicate to win

  - Lots of Class combos

  - Respawn system

  - Always together

Which mechanic detracted the most from the cooperative experience? Negative things.

- Planetside 2

  - Difficult to find your own squad!

  - Hard to stay together (the game didn’t encourage it at all)

  - Minimal interaction with your squad

  - Every location looks the same, minimal landmarks

  - Just another peasant in the mob

  - Incredibly long progression loop, not much feeling of success

  - Multiple people cooperating does not make them more effective.

  - Squads are more powerful because of numbers, not because of class combos.

  - New players start below the Skill Floor

- Splitgate

  - Not-cooperative

  - Environment design is very similar

  - Hard to find others, or describe locations to them. Friction in giving orders?

  - 

- Killing Floor

  - Not so much coop mechanics

- Team Fortress 2

  - Since team actions are so clear, you don’t need to communicate so much.

  - Lack of class combos (medic + everyone, but no other ones)

  - Always the same. Waves come. Go to Shop. Nothing changes.

- Vermintide

  - Limited team choices to make together.

  - The way the Progression system is designed gets in the way.

  - Team Building strategy is only for Late Game players.

Concepts that all games had in common?

- Designed for 4-5 players

- Punished for trying to go solo

- Basic gameloop

- Simple core gunplay. No complex weapon simulation, cover systems, ect.

- Predictable, no randomisation, no surprises.

Observations on the Look and Feel for each game

- Planetside 2

  - Harsh

  - Not Juicy/Utilitarian/Dry

  - Sound effects were mediocre

  - Aged badly

- Splitgate

  - Lighter/Bright

  - Sound effects were ok

  - Halo-esque

  - Characters blend with background

  - HUD UI was clean and interesting. High budget

  - Environment design

  - Gun sound was really meh

- Killing Floor

  - Cheap

  - Guns felt very wet

- Team Fortress 2

  - Sound was more interesting than the other games

  - Color and Variety

  - Very distinguishable classes, environments, weapons, enemies

  - Aged very well

- Vermintide

  - Juicy, Fun

  - Grimdark

  - Color and Variety

  - Important stuff has emission

Golden rules? Theories?

- Having more players should always make your team more powerful.

- Fixed strategy is not enough: You shouldnt be able to autopilot your choices.

- Multiple ways to solve each problem. Not just triggers to push a solution button.

- Randomization might be very important.
