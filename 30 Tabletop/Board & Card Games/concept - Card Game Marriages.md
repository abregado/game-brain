Concept – Card game Marriage mechanic in other genres

A marriage is a common game mechanic in many card games. The player is rewarded for disadvantaging himself by holding onto powerful cards in the hope of getting sets of powerful cards, making them either more powerful, or giving instant rewards.

An Example of marriages from Schnapchen:

Kings and Queens are of middling power in this game. They are useful to the player because he/she can discard them to lose a trick without suffering too much. However if the player holds of on using them, and later gains the matching suited king or queen, he/she is rewarded with a large amount of game points. This ability is countered by either player being able to “close” the deck, forbidding marriages until the next round.

Genres

Deathmatch FPS

The players must all start with several weapons of various grades. Ammunition is full for all weapons to begin with. The players must make kills but also receive points if they have left over ammunition in the more power weapons. Easy kills must be weighed off with the points gained from keeping the good ammunition.

Alternatively players could have access to special powers (Speed, Toughness, Strength, enhanced senses) that are activated with the special ammunition. These would enhance the chances of a player making a kill or surviving, but ultimately cost the player points to use. The winner in this game would be the most efficient killer, not the most successful.

At some point any player can shut down the ability of all players to use special ammunition and abilities. Players must use these abilities early and efficiently to get ahead, then stay in the lead with regular equipment.

RTS

Each player begins play with a number of units, varying in strength. They vie to control land area and must hold the most after a certain time limit. Certain units are much more powerful and can be used to hold large areas of land. At regular intervals, all sides receive reinforcements. If powerful units are held back, they can be “melded” with the more powerful reinforcements to create massivly powerful weapons. The downside is that this can only happen inside ones deployment zone/Landing zone.

Each time reinforcements arrive, both players can add value to a pool. Once this pool reaches a high enough number reinforcements stop coming and new meldings cannot be made.

The winner will be the player who commits just the right amount of units early on and later melds enough more powerful units to take the advantage. Players who use their powerful units and then attempt to withdraw them to meld may find it impossible by the time they have returned to the LZ.

Players who are losing will be closer to the LZ and such can take advantage of melding much faster. This should supply a buffer to the balance between winning and losing, as well as promote the opportunity for late game comebacks.

4X

(Single Player Idea) Each time a player colonizes a planet the first phase of development involves scanning, surveying and searching the planet for useful materials. The player then spends resources and wealth on developing that world to take advantage of the discovered resources. If later on a new resource is discovered, the player cannot use existing infrastructure and development to exploit the new resource.

As technology increases, more powerful resources are available to be discovered. Players will not want to invest heavily early on as they will also need to access the newer materials.

This was also seen in Civilization 4 in the resource placements. If a player places his cities correctly however, it is almost assured that he will gain access to all resources. This also helps new points of conflict develop as the game progresses.
