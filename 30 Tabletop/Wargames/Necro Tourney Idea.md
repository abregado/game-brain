# Idea 1

Problems

- The bidding round requires that everyone show up on time. One late person slows the whole event.

- No incentive to fight someone with nothing that you want.

- You might get forced to fight someone that has nothing you want

This is designed for any wargame that doesnt have its own Tournament system built in. While Necromunda has a campaign system for linking battles it does not have a built in Tournament system for determining a winner after a set number of battles.

Instead of running a narrative campaign like last time, I will be indulging my need for Fluff manufacture on the setting and story. These will not effect the games at all, only what things are called. Examples would include Territory and Hired Gun names, backstories for various items, and a creative licence when I'm writing up battle reports. The Tournament system should allow me to focus my story telling on the story itself without effecting everyone’s gameplay.

This is also a Trial by Fire for future war game tournaments. This system should hold up regardless of the game we choose to play with it.

**Number of Participants**

This Tournament system is designed for 6 or more players. Realistically I can only fit 3 games in my house at once.

**Number of Battles**

Wanting to keep the Tournament to a realistic timeframe, I've roughed it in at 6 sessions. Each player fights once in each session. It could be done with more battles, especially if players can dedicate enough time each session to play multiple times.

**Structure**

The first 3 (or half of the battles) are the Build Up. During these battles, players are rewarded with a number of Victory Points (whether they win or lose), shown by a Location.

During the second half of the battles(aka Warfare), instead players who win may swap one Location with their opponent (winners choice).

This leads to players getting the hang of their gang in their first 3 battles and gives them time to learn of their opponents. By half way, everyone still has a chance to win. The second half of the tournament is by far the most important in terms of winning.

**Bidding for Battles (Build Up)**

At the start of each session, all players sit together and the order of battle is calculated. The player who is currently losing always may chose either his Target, or an Opponent. If he chose an opponent then that player chooses which location to fight for. If a location is chosen then Bidding continues to the next highest scoring player.

If an opponent elects to fight someone who has already selected a Target, they must also fight for that Target.

Targets are made up of two Locations. One is very high in score, one is very low. The largest Location is always paired with the smallest Location (see below)

**Bidding for Battles (Warfare)**

At the start of each of the sessions in the second half of the tournament, the players sit together in the same way as during the Build Up. Players cannot select a location to attack, instead they must select another player.

Once a player has been chosen or has chosen another player, neither player can chose any more players. This means that the three players with the lowest score will decide the order of battle.

After a battle in a Warfare session, the winner may trade a single Location with his opponent (winners choice).

**Locations/Targets**

During each session of the build up, Locations increase in their value.

**Other Ideas**

I thought about incentives to go for combinations of lesser Locations as well. The numbers seem good if I give a bonus for people who collect unfavorable combos. Will play test this more also.

Also I thought about players being able to challenge/interrupt a player in the bidding round. They could steal a players choice if they offer to do so with “one level of disadvantage”. The original bidding player could still take the choice with “two levels of disadvantage”. These would be different for each game, but for Necromunda would include: Scenario Choice, members miss the battle, fail your first X ammo rolls, deployment bonuses, forfeit income, ect.
