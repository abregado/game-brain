These rules are designed to allow players who cannot commit to an entire ACES campaign to play in a drop in, drop out style with their own mechs, and to encourage newer players to join in without the need for owning any figures, while retaining the “skin in the game” feeling of a campaign.

Sponsored Pilots are mercs who have a reduced roster of mech choices, but are not beholden to repair and ammunition costs of a full ACES merc company.

**Designing a Sponsored Pilot**

Take a Sponsored Pilot log book. Choose a Callsign for the pilot and spend 150 SP as you would in a normal ACES campaign. The options for SP upgrades are the regular Pilot Skill, Edge Abilities, Edge Tokens and a new upgrade called Mechbay, which is explained later.

After choosing SP upgrades, select two mechs and add them to your Mechbay. One of these may be worth up to 60 PV, the other up to 20 PV. Fill out their stats in the Log book. You should have enough miniatures that you can field every mech in your mech bay. No two mechs in a Sponsored Pilots mech bay should be of the same model eg:// You cannot have two Warhammers, or two Mad Cats.



**During the Reconnaissance Step of mission setup**

All players decide together which Reconnaissance options are taken. They should be made aware that they may be liable to pay for the cost from the SP gained during this mission, if the Secondary Objectives are not reached.

**During the Player Force step of mission setup**

Each player fielding a Sponsored Pilot selects which mech from their mech bay they will use for the battle.

Players should be able to be reasonable and decide which mechs they each take. If there are disagreements, then use a priority system.

Players have choice priority in the following order:

- Players who are playing for their first time in the campaign (eg:// Brand new Sponsored Pilot)

- Players with no mechs in their mechbay (they choose a mech from the merc force list)

- A player who chooses a cheaper mech

If a player would ever make a choice of mech that would cause another player to be unable to play, then they must choose something with a lower PV cost.

A player may never select a mech that was used in the last mission that the Pilot participated in. Check their mission log to see which mech was used last time.

If there is remaining PV after all participating Sponsored Pilots have chosen their mech, select mechs from the campaign mech force. These extra mechs never have Named Pilots.

Select one Sponsored Pilot to be the mission Commander.

**Before the mission begins**

Players fill in a log entry for the mission, leaving the Kills, SP Earned unfilled, and the MVP, Wounded and Truly Destroyed icons uncolored.



**During the mission**

Sponsored Pilots are considered for all situations to be Named Pilots. The mech of a Sponsored Pilot can only be controlled by the player who owns the Sponsored Pilot.

If a Sponsored Pilot scores the killing blow, they should mark it in the Kills column of that mission’s log entry.

**During the Check for Casualties step**

Determine if your mech is Truly Destroyed

Make a salvage roll if your mech is Destroyed. Each Truly Destroyed pip filled while piloting this mech gives -1 to the roll.

When rolling for wounded crew, the roll is -1 for each Injury the pilot has suffered during the campaign, and -1 if the mech was Truly Destroyed. If a pilot becomes wounded (4-6 on the table) they instead mark an Injury in the log book for this mission. They do not have to skip the next sortie.

The mechs of Sponsored Pilots are not counted towards the repair/rearming costs of the mech force.

If a mech is Truly Destroyed, color in the destroyed icon for this mission's log entry.

**During Calculate Income Step**

All mechs are fully repaired.

**During Improving Named Pilots step**

Each Sponsored pilot that is alive gains half the maximum SP for the mission.

If the secondary objective is complete, pilots gain half the maximum SP for the mission.

If the secondary objective is not complete, pilots collectively pay an equal share for the Reconnaissance costs, up to half the maximum SP for the mission, and then may keep the remaining SP, if any.

Award MVP by player vote. The MVP Pilot colors in the Trophy icon of this mission’s log entry and is awarded 20 SP.

All Sponsored Pilots then allocate SP.

Sponsored Pilots may store unspent SP between missions, but can only spend it after a mission during the Improving Named Pilots step.

**The Mech bay upgrades**

Sponsored Pilots may upgrade their personal mech arsenal. Each level of Mechbay has a different effect.

Level 1: The Pilot immediately adds a third mech to their Mechbay. This mech can be worth up to 30 PV.

Level 2: The Pilot’s second mechbay slot can now hold a mech that costs up to 40 PV. If there is already a mech in this slot, they can replace it with a new mech.

Level 3: The Pilots primary mechbay slot can now hold a mech that costs up to 70 PV. If there is already a mech in this slot, they can replace it with a new mech.



**Omni mechs and Sponsored Pilots**

A pilot who has an omni mech in one of their mechbay slots can choose to pilot any other variant of that mech that costs less PV. Write down the exact model used for a mission in the log book. The campaign mech force does not pay anything to do this. When making a salvage roll, -1 is applied for each variant used after the first over the course of the campaign. This represents extra demand for parts and ammunition causing issues with maintaining the mech.
