## What we will do differently 

**Many D6 pool:** This game uses simple dice, and minimal modifiers. Normal attacks see the players rolling 3+ dice each time, rather than consecutive rolls of a single dice. How the player spends their pool affects how the enemies react.

**Activation order matters.** The enemy can react between activations, so it’s important to plan which actions you take AND with whom you take them very carefully.

**Micro-reactions.** Rolling dice causes enemies to make small actions. Either movements or token placement or aggression. The player can make choices about how they use their dice to change the opponents dice.

**Micro behaviours:** Enemies spend dice in different ways. Highest first, Lowest first, ascending, descending, closest dice to a doorway, pinkest dice first.

**Cover affects behaviour not dice.** You can't target a suppressed target that has cover to you. Suppressed targets in cover act differently.

**Living rulebook.** Print is available, but the rules are always online and free.

**Force sheet print ready:** Cards are crap, but supported if you want. If a units abilities dont fit on a single side of a playing card, they are too long. A force should fit on an A4 sheet. One side for units, one for faction rules.

**Ability blockers clearer: To avoid repetition of text** abilities are marked if they are deactivated when in control range or outside control range. Passives are either on or off. Actions are either available or unavailable. Ability targets are either self, enemy, friendly or friendly faction.

**Fit the rules to the format:** We design the rules to match the UX, not the other way around. Rules are either Passives, Actions, Faction, Weapon. Trigger points are clear and limited to a few places. Abilities only trigger on those moments. Weapon rules are reused across all weapons. No faction only weapon rules. Weapon rules only trigger during the attack rolls or resolution.

**Token signalling:** actions place tokens on units, which can be converted by other actions or change/disappear at the end of the round. Tokens have effects or can trigger weapon effects.

**Agility via straight line movement:** All movement must be made in straight lines. More agile units have multiple smaller moves. A unit might be fast, but not agile.

**Cover:** A unit is in cover or not. If a unit is in cover, the effect of that cover is based on the units passive abilities. Some units cannot benefit from cover. The check should be reversed, since every player will want their units in cover. Instead you are not in cover if it is clear that you are not in cover. Otherwise you are in cover.

**Enemies are more than stat blocks:** Every enemy has minor behavioural changes, not just different dice targets.

**Focus on terrain effects:** Level design is important to video games, why not to tabletop games as well.

**Grim and expressive:** Your guys are going to get chunks blown out of them, but that is just another opportunity for growth and character development.

**3x3 foot (1x1m) board size or smaller:** The action starts immediately. No single turn of moving up and positioning. Set up is much more important. 2x2 would even be better.

**Better system for Interrupt abilities:** These should be presented as a special type of action and all operate the same way to reduce repetition. Hearthkyn Field Medics “Medic!” ability.

**Suppression.** Suppression tokens are added to NPCs even on failure. Players need to attempt something against as many NPCs as possible. We can keep NPCs' actions lethal if they are unsuppressed. Hopefully this makes the player feel like they chose the result when that NPC nukes a PC with their plasma cannon.

**Reactions**: If a unit is fired upon, it can spend its activation to do something. The set of actions are reduced, and they can only spend 1 AP on doing something. EG://

**Hunker Down:** Cover Effectiveness increases until next activation.

**Dive**: move the unit 2” then give it a Prone token.

**Snap Shot:** immediately after the attack is made, this unit can return fire but only with 2 dice. Meat Shield: Move another unit within line of sight so its base touches this unit. That unit is now the target of the attack.

Which reaction is available is based on the unit type and the weapon specials.

Random scenario

Prepare the random enemy chart based on which figures you have

Decide a primary objective

Each player selects half of their Killteam

Place objectives

Place one 12” marker next to each objective

Roll on your prepared random enemy chart. Take the figures and set them aside.

Place one of the position markers next to the models.

Each player places one of the other two matching position markers within 6” of the objective, and more than 8” away from each other.

Roll again on the prepared random enemy chart. And complete the marker placement, this time within 12” of the objective. These markers cannot be within 8” of any other marker.

Roll twice on the prepared random enemy chart. Each player places two of the rolled markers exactly 3” away from an existing marker.

Randomize which objective is the primary and what the secondaries are.

The players deploy their units. They must deploy further than 6” away from any marker.

Determine randomly, which marker each enemy unit spawns on.

All units which are not visible to an enemy unit, or are visible but are in cover, start with Conceal orders. All others start with engage orders.

Play begins.

Coop Scenario ideas

Models on ground cannot fire at models on high ground at all. Move across the battlefield. All models on high ground can see all models on high ground.

When an activation ends, any player model is within 4” of a portal and there are no enemies within 18” of the centre point, all models within 4” of a portal are moved to the other side of the board.

Two large units are fighting. The large units prioritize each other unless the player team has dealt one of them more damage than the other large unit. Track who caused damage. Both large units must die with the players having dealt at least half the damage to that unit.

Platforms moving up and down. Red platforms start at 0 and go up to 6, blue platforms start at 6 and go down to 1.

Teleporters scattered around the map. Enter a teleporter and suffer a random malus to teleport to any other teleporter. Every time an enemy dies, randomize which teleporter is active. Once the objective is half achieved, deactivate an additional random teleporter each time it happens. Operatives get a free activation after being teleported. Random set of cards after board is set up to determine where teleporters and the centre point are placed. If the new board needs to be exited, the initial board is destroyed. 2 or 4 boards of 2x2 ft? Single kill team board per board.

6 points on the map need to be accessed. You can deploy each of your units within 2” of any point. Activating a point takes either 1 or 2 turns.

Additional collectables to tempt players to be suboptimal. Give story nuggets at the end for extras they gathered. Make the next scenario harder for each bonus they collected in a mission.

After deployment on an overwhelming board, give the players a number of activations each before the game starts. NPCs do not move or react to these activations.

Place D3 tokens next to each cover terrain piece. When firing upon an enemy in cover, if the shot causes no damage, remove one of the tokens. If there are no tokens left, you may not fire upon targets using that terrain as cover. Each time you defeat an enemy in melee combat, add a token to a terrain piece of your choice. There is an event that causes tokens to be removed on any critical hit from firing against any NPC within 1” of the terrain, even if not in cover.

Killing an enemy causes a random enemy that has already acted to Counteract, moving and then Concealing if in cover.

Default action: move to cover and conceal.

Each shot against an NPO gives it a token. Each token gives it 1 extra defense die. Clear dice when the unit activates.

Two mission actions. One is only for Eldar/Psychic characters (or any smaller sub group)
