## 7 Point adventure plan

[<u>https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=v3Nh4cydutA</u>](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=v3Nh4cydutA)

1.  Hook

2.  Shift 1

3.  Complication 1

4.  Midpoint

5.  Complication 2

6.  Shift 2

7.  Resolution

**Hook**: get the players involved. Threaten something that both the players and characters care about.

**Shift**: An assumption the players had about the situation is not as it seemed.

**Complication**: something introduces conflict or wants to the opposite of the characters.

**Resolution**: The consequences of the story are revealed.

## Hooks

Produce 2-4 hooks that increase in how direct the threat is. A generic example:

1.  There is something interesting over there

2.  The interesting thing is threatening something the characters find important

3.  An antagonistic force is looking for the interesting thing

4.  The interesting thing has already damaged something important

## Shifts

Reveal more information about the situation. Shifts should be knowledge of the future that the PCs cannot avoid. They need to factor them into the plan. Some examples:

- A known faction will be affected positively or negatively, so actions to resolve the situation in a particular way will change their opinion of the characters.

- The consequences for resolving the situation are higher or lower than expected.

- Introduce a moral dilemma, or better a trilemma.

- Knowledge of who caused the situation

- Discovery of the situations place in a larger plot

- Finding out the situation itself has intent

- The area around the resolution site changes the rules.

## Complication/Conflict

Someone or something other than the player characters is going to DO something, or something the PCs do fails to work. Complications must be resolved or avoided in order to resolve the situation. If a complication can just be avoided by knowing about it beforehand, then you have actually created a Shift. Some examples:

- A known faction moves in to resolve the situation themselves.

- The situation is able to defend itself.

- Introduction of a new ongoing faction.

- One piece of the PCs plan fails to work, or goes unexpectedly.

## Midpoint

Generally it is here that players will create their plan.

I want to write an adventure that is suitable for new players and new dungeon masters..

Overall

- Each session is dedicated to either roleplay or combat

- 10 clues are written for the DM to give to the players, which lead them to one of two locations in the next chapter.

- Replayability is important ,so the DM can learn with several groups

- Build in resting locations, write in reasons why the players dont stop to long rest

- Planned exposition,either via NPC’s or objects

Roleplay sessions

- Each session only contains one NPC that the DM must play

- It contains a description of the character

- Every character has an image

Combat sessions

- Each session introduces a different mechanic

- Does not use gridded combat, perhaps until the boss fight

- Every monster has a stat block

- Monsters do flat (non-random) damage

- 2-3 of these encounters before the players can rest

Locations

- Dungeons always have a mythal against one school of magic

- Warn the players about this and let them reselect their spells

- If a resting locations, give a list of what becomes harder in the coming sections

- Have interesting and useful items for sale when the heroes meet a shopkeeper

- Make sure purchased items are relevant for this adventure (at least one obvious usecase)

Rewards

- Treasure locations always list something for each class

- the DM is suggested to give rewards evenly over time so that by the end everyone has similar amounts of items

- Items also have a school, so they can interact with the mythals.

- Interesting treasure, both one use, class ability buffing and passive bonuses

- Dont value treasure until the heroes return to town (give a reward price lookup in the back of the adventure)
