Forge of Fury

Baron Althon, a local noble has hired you to search for evidence of Durgeddin the dwarven smith. He showed you a broken blade bearing a smith's mark, and gave you a rubbing of the impression. A map shows where the blade was found. He will pay you 20gp each just for exploring the area. If there is a hidden dwarven forge then he will pay 400gp for a detailed map of the complex. Any dwarven weapons bearing the smith’s mark will also be richly rewarded.

Direct to Foundry

Intro

Indeed the map was a good lead. After a few days of asking around the local hamlets you ambushed a patrol of orcs and took your first prize, an axe made from a broken sword bearing the smiths mark. Tracking the lone orc you released, you discovered a dwarven ruin high in the mountains. The orcs have been using it as their hideout.

While they were too numerous to engage directly, you easily managed to sneak by and made your way down into the Foundry itself.

**Information to share about Druegar**

They are not evil in the traditional sense, instead they end up looking evil because they take hardline practical stances.

They are practical to a fault.

They hate embellishment or form for the sake of beauty.

An item or person's value comes from their function.

They have no emotion and always take the hyper rational option, even if evil.

They can go invisible for a time

They can enlarge themselves for a time.

Any areas used by the Druegar are cleaned and maintained.

Moral choice at the end

Attacks on local villages by demons draw in the PCs

The source is linked back to a ruin somewhere

PCs fight into the ruins

In the final room they find a very powerful demon trapped by a magic spear.

- The spear prohibits the demon from moving

- Dying on the material plane simply sends him back to the Abyss

- The spear is linked to the material plane so teleports him back here randomly

PCs can fight the demon while it is incapacitated

Or

PCs can remove the spear and fight the fully powered demon with the spear

Druegar heist adventure. Evil Montage adventure.

In Duergar (Dwergar, Duegar, Dögar) society wealth, both material and social, are the metrics for status. While this may seem similar to what you know of regular dwarf society, the truth is much more grim. Complete utilitarianism dominates the average Duergar life. Beauty and form are a waste of energy and effort. Citizens of authority determine every life choice of those beneath them; marriages, births, job allocations. Everything is done to maximise the power of the Duergar. Weak members are considered criminals and the death penalty is easily invoked.

Their citizens live by three rules:

**Our Pockets are Never Full**. The simple act of acquisition is the purest form of experience, if a transient one. To show any sign of contentment or success, including flaunting your wealth is a sin. Progress must continue eternally.

**Our Fight is Never Done.** It is unjust that one may hold wealth without needing to defend it. Ergo it is not only natural, but also just to take from those who cannot defend themselves from you. As acquisition is endless, so should one forever seek power where possible.

**Our Resolve is never shaken.** To show any sign of weakness is a sin. Happiness and friendship are weaknesses. To show signs of fear, doubt or apathy are weaknesses, and therefore sin. Cracks are where the blades of our enemies can find purchase, so the cracks in our society are unacceptable.

“Work or die. In the first case, you are useful. In the second, you are entertaining.” - Duergar poet

“The worst insult you can give a Duergar is to call them Fancy” - Underdark trader

5 Scenes

Intro.

You are a crack team being sent to infiltrate a compound where some filthy sun slaves have uncovered a Netherese portal room. The mission has been in planning for months. You have received good intel about the compound and your route through it has been planned. Get in as quietly as you can, link one of the portals to our stronghold and then activate it. A strike team is standing by to join you on sight as soon as the connection is made.

You arrive by boat on an underground sea. Along the way, several aquatic creatures were too curious, but you dealt with each in turn. Now as you approach your destination, you have extinguished your lantern and attempted to dock your boat safely. An occasional guard patrols a thin wet ledge with regularly spaced Mooring posts jutting from it.

Your sources were good. You slide the secret entrance closed and look out across the room. The sound of rushing water is regularly punctuated by the clang of metal striking metal. Dominating the room is a huge machine which is being fed by cart loads of ore. Tens of workers are climbing around the walkways encircling the machine. A huge water wheel drives the machine and is fed from a channel that runs through the room.

You are all crouched in a corridor behind a thick stone door. On the other side is the next area, a defensive checkpoint, you must pass. You have seen the detailed plans of how it is laid out, and where the guards move. It is a long room with a ballista in the centre facing the far end of the room. On the far side there is a row of wooden spikes. To the right is another door which goes to a barracks where the guards work and take breaks. You need to go through the barracks to reach your next destination.

Approaching the next obstacle you note that the architecture changes. Ahead is a long series of very perfectly clean, smooth walled corridors, 4m wide and 4m high. At regular intervals in this maze, iron grates are fixed in the roof and floor allowing Gelatinous cubes to come and go. These slide silently and almost invisibly along, removing any organic matter and collecting non-organic matter in piles on top of the grates.

After the maze, you enter the mines and head downwards. After a long time in old tunnels which show no sign of use in the last decades, you encounter a fresh excavation. The architecture beyond stinks of the demon witch Lolth and her spider loving offspring. From here the portal room is easy to locate. Although you find it unguarded there are telltale signs that something is inhabiting the area. But the biggest problem. The circle of runes in the centre of the room are not Netherse, but Primordial. This is going to take some time. Four other portal mounts stand at the cardinal points of the room.

You link the portal but something is wrong.
