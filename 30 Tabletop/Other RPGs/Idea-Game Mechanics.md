**Interface ideas –** completely unrelated

Time Based: options only become available for short periods of time. The player must press a shortcut or click the option before it disappears.

Random Element: Several options are grouped together. Selecting the group activates one option randomly. The player must risk the options he/she wishes for being activated.

Pictorial Menu: The player is shown an image and must locate the options available to him/her. EG// A image of a town is shown. The player can click of locations to visit them, however no effort is made to point these out.

**Save Point Ideas –** fixing the problem

*Save point Teleport:* Can only save at save points but can teleport back to these points at any time. Coupled with limited supplies, this would cause areas to become less reachable as they move farther away from the save points.

*Many Save Points:* Can only save at save points however there are many along the way. Dangerous Areas have very few safe points. Players can easily reach new locations, however they cannot use save/load as a strategy to overcome a puzzle.

*Generate on Load:* You can save at any point however every time you load, content is re-generated. Players still lose very little of their journey when they load, but must always face unexpected challenges.

Remove Save/Load: Players have no reason to save unless they wish to quit. Removing all reason for loading would include no position based Death penalty.

**Character “Sheets”** – increasing the mystery

Hidden traits: Skill levels are hidden from the players view. They can perform simple tests to see how well they can perform. They cannot get an exact image of their abilities and receive no numbers to calculate their chances.

Perception skills: These levels determine how accurate a characters assessment of a situation is. They could be called characters “cockiness”.

Writing/Memory Skills: These determine how much information is gathered while playing. Remembered information is always available for later use. Everything else the player must remember.

Identification skills: These determine the characters ability to test and recognise items and their abilities. This coupled with Writing and Memory allows items to be permanently recognised.

**Combat –** its supposed to be scary

Unknown Enemies: You shouldn’t know that you can beat an enemy until you have beaten an enemy. Remembering which enemies you have defeated should be a vital part of the game. Including a tool to allow players to record this in game might be appropriate.

Injuries: These take time to heal and lower your effective travelling speed. More time spent at camp means less exploring gets done. Open wounds have a chance of infection.

**Inventory –** Size AND weight

Options: Players should not have a chance to fill up on supplies very often during an adventure. They must think about what to bring and what to drop when the need arises.

**Character Advancement –** NO EXPEREINCE POINTS OR GRINDING

NO Experience Points: Characters never earn experience points at all. You may never spend points to level up particular skills.

Training: After completing quests, or reaching in game goal areas, you will be invited to visit a Master and complete several training tasks. These each increase your skill levels in different areas based on how well you do. The player will never see exactly how much is gained or in what areas.

Goal Areas: These are points in the game that are very hard to reach for characters. Players will have to know their character very well to gain access to these areas. Once gained, the player can visit the Master for training (See Above)

Gradual Buildup: Characters do not gain a lot from training, but it is enough to tip the balance between success and failure. The highest level character can still be killed by the lowest level monster if they are stupid about it.

Inherent Abilities: When a character is made, the player will not know his skills or abilities. Experimenting with different attacks and weapons will show which the character is inherently skilled with. This will not stop players from continuing a career which is parallel from these inherent abilities.

**Travel Ideas –** making it interesting

*Complex Travel calculator:* Many factors determine how far from a town a player can travel. These include:

Hiking Speed (Size of character/agility/strength)

Difficulty of terrain (Slope, obstacles/footing)

Hunger (hours until food is needed)

Eating Efficiency (Metabolism/Food quality)

Stamina (hours until rest is needed)

Rest Efficiency (Quality of Rest/Location/Watch periods)

Ration weight (Quality)

Carrying capacity (Strength, quality of baggage)

Camp setup time (Quality of equipment, camping skills)

Getting lost (Quality of maps, navigator/guild skills)

*Group Benefits:* Obviously bringing a character with high Strength, another with good cooking skills and one with High Rest Efficiency (to lower guard times) will help the party a great deal. Also seeking out the best equipment and better food ingredients will help as well. Weight and set up times for multi person tents will be better than everyone using their own single man tents.

Concept: Fantasy Role Playing Game. No experience, no levelling, no stat/skill visibility. Player has a description of his characters abilities but must learn its capabilities through personal experience. Characters do not improve very much over their lifetimes. Their power comes from the player exploring their abilities.

Narrative: Comet made of time distorting material fragments and falls to the planet. Time near to the fragments travels slower than further away. Civilization build up around the craters. The wilderness areas degenerate into monster infested wilderness.

Gameplay: Players start with a randomised character. They can select gender and select a name from the name generator. The characters innate abilities are pre-determined but hidden from the player.

The player first has access to the academy grounds, where he can trial his character in perfect safety and spar with other players. Once the player feels confident with his characters abilities he can venture out into the wilderness surrounding the town.

Training and Travelling: Characters cannot increase in skill until they reach the next major location. This location is outside the easy reach of characters unless the player knows their skills very well. It should still be difficult for parties of characters to reach. Once here, this new location can be used as a Save Point. Training consists of completing a number of tasks or quests that will give small boosts to characters skills.

Rewards: As players progress they will increase the quality of their equipment. The benefits these give are minor over regular equipment, but will require less maintenance. Low quality equipment will break when under stress; high quality equipment will give the player some piece of mind, allowing him to journey farther from settlements.

Scoring and Measuring Success: As characters complete quests and kill monsters, they will fill a Journal. This serves as a record of the achievements of that character. Characters with a lot of achievements in their lifetime have their Journals added to the Great Library once they die. They also have statues and tapestries made in their honour which are littered about the city.

Penalties, Death and Saving: When a player is killed, he/she is immediately resurrected at the last visited settlement. Characters also age, and will eventually die. Being resurrected will add a number of years to their age based on the severity of their death. They can eventually purchase chunks of meteor to wear to slow down the aging process. This cannot imbue immortality. When a character reaches late middle age, his stats will begin to deteriorate, however his/her skills will remain strong if they are used constantly.

Content Buffers: To further from masses of Comet a hero gets, the faster he will age. To gain access to all content in the game, he will have to sacrifice lifespan to explore the furthest territories. The likelihood of a character exploring all of the outer territories in one lifetime is zero. Replaying the game is vital to the game play experience.

Player Skill: The time it takes for a player to familiarize himself with a new character is vital to the total amount of playtime he will get with that character. Learning to read the hidden information based on responses from the game world is the primary way to become “good” at the game.

Player Fear: This concept is necessary to holding back players from committing whole heartedly to battle and should generate some non-programmed “morale” effects in battles. Players realise they have limited time and for once their choices close off options that might have been available to them later on. Time pressure is vital for generating a whole host of interesting human responses in a game world.

Procedurally Generated Game world: Due to enhanced speed, the movement and evolution of creature groupings can be seen throughout a single characters lifespan. Returning to town for a single day could see months of movement in a pack of wolves just a few hundred miles away. Even further out, evolution or eradication of new species could happen within a single year of “town time”. No matter how far the heroes explore, a large portion of the game world will be evolving without them. Characters can explore for months on end, knowing that money and equipment kept in the town will be completely safe for their return.
