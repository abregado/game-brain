Concept – City Builder with industrial touch (process interdependancy)

A multiplayer game for 3 or more players. Semi-cooperative.

Players build industrial cities using a detailed, interdependant industry system. They may export or import commodities to/from other players. There are limited raw materials in the game world. Currency is not used, instead players must chose which mineral they use as their “basis of comparison”

Direct competition is not the only way to foil your opponents plans. Working cooperativly can benefit you slightly more than the other player. Players making deals will be completely nessesary as it will be impossible to cover all elements of the industrial flow chart and still be competative.

Thematic/Setting ideas

Asteroid Mining on one large asteroid

Resources are Ores, Air, He, Water, Special minerals, Solar energy, Area

Asteroid mining in a belt

Resources are same as above but more importance placed on area

Competing space stations in orbit around one planet

Limited resources come from the planet

Processes convert materials only

Spending resources to increase the size of your station

Competing Villages (Steampunk or Fantasy)

Resources are magical reagents, creatures, crops, treasure

Competing Colonies on a new world (Domed Cities)

Resources are similar to real world, but also area, HE, Water, Air

Perhaps periodical resources are brought from outside the system and sold to the highest bidder. These could be “Tech level” enhancing items that are unavailable inside the game system.

Example:

There are 4 new colonies founded on a new planet. Each begins harvesting the local resources and converting them into basic supplies. One specialises in food, trading it for equipment from another colony. The other two are self sufficent in these items but produce small quantities of a more advanced resource that they trade for ores. Every few years a starship comes brining a supply of high tech items from Earth. The colonies bid for the stocks, the winner being the colony who wants to most items at the highest price. These enable a colony to produce on a much higher level, or can be resold at much higher prices to the other colonies.

Late Game:

As players build up their industrial chains and a series of deals with the other players, their industry becomes like a house of cards. More and more dependancies are stacked on top of one another, creating weak links in the supply chain. Players must exploit these links in their opponents chains and cause them to fail, or use problems generated to drive up the price of needed goods (in turn generating extra wealth for those who provide said goods).

Notes to self when designing industrial flowcharts.

- Resources should be sorted into “tiers” to make it easier to understand

- Every process should require resources from at least 2 tiers

- Every resource should be required for at least 2 processes

- “Factories” should be capable of running several different processes, but one at a time

- Retooling costs should be paid each time a new batch job is designed

- Cancelling a job should cause expensive setbacks

- Building factories should require a few resources from a higher tier

- Building factories should be expensive almost to the point of irreversible

- The highest tier items should contain all of the base resources (through the lower tier items)

- Efficiency of conversion should be different for each process ingredient

- Some higher processes should make earlier processes redundant, but more risky
