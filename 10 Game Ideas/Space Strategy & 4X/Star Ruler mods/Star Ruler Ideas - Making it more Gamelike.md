Planetary Condition Grouping

Each planet can only have one effect from each catagory:

Atmospheric

Weather

Geographical

Biosphere

Correct combinations would make a world very profitable, while most combinations make it almost uninhabitable.

Conditions also increase Damage Reduction and Maximum hit of facilities on the planet.

Make Homeworlds on a different scale!

Distance Limitations

The further form the Homeworld a planet is, the faster its people become unhappy

The further from the Homeworld a planet is, the lower the bandwidth of Galactic Bank Transfers.

These could be system wide effects shown in the stars Hover information. Calculated with the distance between this star and the home star.

Defensibility

When less than a certain number of ships are in a system, the Piracy rating goes up. This effects the efficiency of the galactic bank (a portion of transferred funds are stolen)

Groups of stars closer to each other will make it easier to defend with less ships.

Number of primary planets to defend in each system

Homeworld Changes:

Homeworlds should have a number of modifiers to make them totally different form other worlds. The first would be to change population growth so that it is % based. This would make the homeworld replenish population MUCH faster than other worlds.

Secondly the Homeworlds should have a modifier to Damage Resistance. I would call this trait “Pre-Warp Infrastructure”. It would give the planet a 600% bonus to Damage Resisitance, a 300% modifier to City Size and a 500% modifier to construction costs. In the early game, upgrading and building new facilities should be not as high priority as founding new colonies, however money spent on the homeworld should be a sound investment.

Lastly I would have “excess population” go into a galactic pool which building up (just like the galactic bank). This would represent the empire wide “emigration”. Colonies without max population receive this population to supplement their main population growth. Each amount of people shipped costs a few units of metal. If the metal reserve is empty, these people build up. A high number of population “in limbo” generate a negative moral effect on the Homeworld.

In the late game, the Homeworld becomes less central to your empire as other colonies will rise up to match its power. Obviously these will require much more defense as they are not hardended from the outset like the homeworld.

Immigration Costs

The cost to “import” spare population is goverened by the quality of the planet. A harsh planet will cost a lot to have people immigrate to. A paradise world with little ore might be used simply to breed new colonists!

Intended Strategy Changes

Early Game, players will need to consider the investment cost of each world. Searching the galaxy wide to find suitable planets. The “value” of two mediocre planets in the same system may very well be much higher than two better planets in separate systems. The long and short term goals of your empire will determine your first planet choice. Place your first colony on a planet with a Research penalty and you may find yourself behind in tech!

Mid game, players will have to decide if more weapons tech is more important than improving their economy tech. Some techs should probably modify the Build Cost Penalties of building on bad planets. As quality planets will be more spread out, Mobile Defense fleets will be much better value over stationary stations.

Late Game, high cost and high defense bonus planets will become strongholds while your pre-developed worlds can be slagged instantly from orbit. Also these planets will be the only ones available with Ore available.

Discussion of Wanted Outcome

Majority of planets should be:

Expensive to Develop (Cost Modifier)

Slow to grow (Population Growth/Housing)

Those with large bonuses should most often have high costs as well. Rarely there should be a planet with a high Bonus but no Cost penalty.
