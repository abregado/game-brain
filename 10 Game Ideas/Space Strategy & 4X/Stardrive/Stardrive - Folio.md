Stardrive

This project has been part of my life since 1996. Part of me knows it will never see the magical light of a department store shelf. Another part of me wishes that it will some day. Either way, every time I see new games that give players control over the content I think back and hope.

History of Stardrive

Tabletop Beginnings (1996)
Stardrive began as a rules modification for a tabletop game called Full Thrust. The game itself was great fun; however I always felt that the players were forced to use the same type of ships and tactics in every game.

It dawned on me that players should be able to create their own weapons, ships and defensive systems for use in the game. This would diversify our battles which had been reduced to slugfests between the now well known "best ship in the game".

Of course, some guidelines would be needed to keep game balance and I created a whole new layer of rules that players could use to generate their own Empire/Species/Race of aliens, the ships they flew and their tactics.

Species creation gave 8 fields with 8 options in each field. This gave my players over a million different options for exploring new ideas. The new rules took off and before long I was tapping into the previously stagnant pool of my players imaginations.

Lesson 1: Players CAN have control over the content if you give them guidelines

Campaign (1998)
After the success of my previous modification, I created yet another layer to Stardrive. This would be the empire management component that gave players reason and stories behind their battles.

The unfortunate truth of this campaign was that I wrote it with far too much complexity for a pen & paper game. Not only that but it seemed that the players became more interested in developing their empires and less in playing the tabletop game it was designed to encourage.

So instead of creating more exciting and well linked table top battles, it stopped them completely. Even tho all players enjoyed this campaign only 5 battles ever took place.

This pushed Stardrive firmly into the realm of a 4X game.

Lesson 2: If a mistake turns into a success, roll with it

Online Campaign (1999-2000)
Following increased interest in a campaign similar to the one previously run I set about creating an online version. The game itself was run through a Bulletin Board forum hosted by one of the player. Complexity of the game was increased, and the focus was shifted away from space battles toward more diplomatic combat.

Many flaws were generated by this move. Players could contact me a lot easier, access information regarding their empire instantly, and communicate with other players at their will. However, the game itself was still being run on paper with very little help from a computer. A friend and I developed a very complex spreadsheet for keeping track of assets and this sufficed for a few months until the amount of data became unmanagable. The campaign fell apart shortly after my own workload reached about 4 hours a day (I was also studying VCE at the time).

Lesson 3: Keep It Simple Stupid, unless you can program a PC to do it for you!

Reborn (2005)
Stardrive went on the backburner for the next 5 years. I still wrote down concepts when I thought about it and I now have a very healthy wad of paper scraps with ideas all over them. I contemplated the successful parts of the past and likelihood of achieving anything else with my dream project in the future.

Very few commitments during 2005 led me to pick up the original tabletop idea. I decided to polish it and if all went well to publish it on the web for others to read. Although I did polish the game, I never did publish it anywhere. This final project made me realise how much I love having a dream. Stardrive is by pet, my unachievable goal, and a symbol of my own quest for the perfect game in an imperfect world.

Lesson 4: Its important to have unachievable goals. It makes all the other ones seem easier to reach

Lesson 5: Never give your IP away for free!

The Dream

Stardrive lives on in my head and in a huge pile of scrap paper I keep filed away for the day when it can be achieved. Perhaps ill have a grandchild that will finish the project.

If you would like to hear more about Stardrive just ask me, but be sure to bring snacks.
