###  **Introduction**

The Stardrive tabletop system is written as a rules modification of Full Thrust, a game designed by Jon M. Tuffley. Any ideas that seem similar are so because I have taken a lot from the original and modified it to create what I always hoped was a far more tactical game. There are two concepts I have tried to keep consistent throughout my writings. Firstly I have tried to improve upon the game elements that make ship placement more important. You should now find that undefended Dreadnoughts are a lot easier to take out with your Escorts (provided you have the right weapons fitted).

The second concept is the one of game complexity. I am a firm believer that I can create an extremely deep and complex ship design system while keeping the actual battle simple and fluid. I hope you will agree I have succeeded once you have played your first game.

# **Basic Game Concepts**

## ***Mass and Ship Classes***

Mass is the measurement used to represent the size of a star ship. All Military Spacecraft are categorized using their Mass rating to three groups. Escorts are the smallest ships, ranging from Mass 2 to Mass 18. Cruisers are the middling class, having decent speed and payloads. These have a Mass of 19-38. Capitals are the largest ships, packing incredible defensive and offensive capabilities, but lacking speed. Any ship of Mass 39 or more is considered capital.

## ***Thrust and Move***

All ships in Stardrive have both a Thrust and Move rating. Thrust measures the ships ability to increase and decrease its speed between manoeuvres while Move shows how much Thrust can be spent on changing the ships heading per turn. Escort ships have a maximum of Thrust 8 and Move 3. Cruiser may have up to Thrust 6 and Move 2 and Capitals may only have Thrust 2 and Move 1.

## ***Velocity***

While Thrust is a ships ability to increase and decrease speed, it is worth noting that a ships maximum Velocity is infinite. The movement in Stardrive is designed to reflect a Newtonian model. Just remember that while accelerating to incredible speeds is possible, it is not recommended.

## ***Fire Arcs***

Each weapon fitted to a ship may have one or more arcs. These are chosen at the design stage and cannot be changed during combat.

### **Compass Arcs**

These basic arcs are used to determine damage location as well as being used for most of the basic weapons in Stardrive. Relative to any single ship, a Hex can never be in two of these arcs at the same time.

\<\<DIAGRAM\>\>

### **Forward Arc**

This arc extends from the centre of the Hex immediately in front of the ship out 20 degrees to each side.

### **Port/Starboard Arcs**

Both of these arcs extend outwards from the two Hexes immediately to the left or right of the ship.

### **Rear Arc**

Shaped exactly like the Forward Arc, this arc extends from the Hex immediately behind the ship.

## ***Advanced Arcs***

### **Turret Arc**

This arc covers every angle around the ship. Think of this as a combination of the Forward, Rear, Port and Starboard arcs.

### **Cannon Arc**

This arc starts from the same hexes as the Front arc but only extends in a three hex Line directly in front of the ship. Most of the heavier weapons have this arc.

## ***Range Bands***

Range is always measured as the shortest number of hexes between two points. Range bands are grouped into sets of 6 Hexes. Range 1 is any point within 6 Hexes, Range 2 is any point within 12 Hexes, ect.

## ***Damage Types***

### **Beam Damage**

Most of the basic weapons are listed as causing a number of Beam Damage. This is written in their summaries as 2b or 5b, ect. Always roll Beam damage separately from Direct Damage. After calculating the number of Beam Damage dice that being inflicted, roll them all together and consult this chart.

| Result | Damage    |
|--------|-----------|
| 1-3    | No damage |
| 4-5    | 1 Damage  |
| 6      | 2 Damage  |

### **Direct Damage**

Direct damage is utilized by several of the higher class weapons available. When rolling for Direct Damage, the result on the Dice is the amount of damage inflicted. Weapons with this type of damage hve it listed as a number followed by “d” (2d or 4d).

# **Turn Order**

## ***Orders Phase***

During this phase, each player places movement cards next to each of their ships. Movement cards are placed face down so that the movement is not revealed to other players until the next phase. Once all ships have been accounted for (double check any ships that don’t have movement cards next to them) flip over all placed cards and start the next phase.

### **Velocity Change**

Velocity Changes are always factored in BEFORE any subsequent manoeuvres. This is critical for smaller ships as it allows them to react very quickly to a dynamic battlefield.

### **Placing Multiple Manoeuvre Cards**

When placing several move cards, each is calculated in the order you place them. Also, your ships current Velocity is shared out between any cards you select.

Example: If your ship had a Velocity of 4 last turn and you Accelerate by 4 and place 2 left turn Move cards down, each of the Move cards should be calculated at Velocity 4. The ship will move Forward 2 hexes, turn left, then move Forward 4 hexes, make another turn then finish by moving forward another 2 hexes.

## ***Ship Movement***

### **Regular Ship Movement**

Starting with the largest craft, move each ship as is specified on the movement cards its owning player has chosen. Once a ship has been moved, remove its cards from the table so that everyone knows its movement has been calculated.

### **Fighters and Missile Movement**

Once all ships have been moved, its time to move any Fighters and Missiles that are active on the battlefield. Fighters do not have a heading and may freely change direction during their movement. Missiles are incredibly fast and while guided, often overshoot their targets, especially if you are aiming them at Escorts! To move missiles simply announce how many hexes it will move this turn and whether it will turn left, right or continue straight. If a missile turns, the turn is made in the middle of its movement.

If a missile moves into a hex adjacent to an enemy craft it will detonate during the gunnery phase. Fighters may attack during the gunnery phase if they are adjacent to an enemy ship.

## ***Gunnery Phase***

All gunnery in Stardrive is considered Simultaneous. This means that damage is only applied during the Logisitics Phase. As such, the order in which your ships fire is not important as they will get to fire even if destroyed.

When firing, select a ship and designate which enemy ships it will fire upon. A ship may target one enemy craft for each Fire Control it has fitted. Once targets have been chosen, select which weapons will be fired at each one. A ship may fire all of its weapons during each gunnery phase.

Each group of weapons fired at a target is considered a single Attack.

## ***Logistics Phase***

During the Logistics Phase, damage from attacks made during the Gunnery Phase is applied to each ship. Damage is marked on the ships Record Sheet.

Once damage has been calculated, ships may recover partially by using Shield Generators and Repair Crews. Each Shield Generator allows one Shield Point to be added to the ships shields. Only Arcs that currently have less than their maximum Shield Points may be added to.

Each Repair Crew allows a single roll to be made to repair a damaged system. Allocate all Repair crews before rolling a single D6 for each one. If any of the rolls for a single system come up as 6, the system is repaired.

#  **System Explanations**

## ***Offensive Systems***

### **Beam Batteries**

Each of the beam batteries (C, B, A, AA) cover the front and side arcs of a ship. Each type inflicts a number of Beam Damage based on range. The maximum damage for each type is:

C (1b), B (2b), A (3b), AA (4b)

As range increases, the effectiveness of beams decrease. Each battery loses one dice of Beam damage for each additional range band.

*Example: B Batteries cause 2b damage at range 1 (1-6 hexes), and 1b damage at range 2 (7-12 hexes)*

### **S Battery**

This is the largest beam weapon available. It may only be mounted on Capital class ships. S Batteries cause 6b damage at Range 1, with a maximum range of 4. However whenever rolling beam damage using an S Battery, if more than one “1” is rolled, the Beam burns out and must be repaired by the Crew.

### **Submunitions Launcher**

Submunitions work similar to Beam Batteries. They cause 3b damage at Range one but suffer double falloff penalites (so cause 1b at Range 2). Each time a Submunitions Launcher is used, cross off one unit of ammunition from your ship record sheet. In addition Submunitons ignore the effects of Screens.

### **Torpedo Launcher**

Torp Launchers are one of the more effective weapons at damaging well defended capital ships. They cause 1d (direct damage) to their target and therefore ignore the effects of Screens. However they are ineffective at hitting manoeuvrable targets. Roll one dice and score higher than the targets Move rating to hit. Add 2 to the targets effective Move rating for each Range Band above 1 (+2 for Range 2, +4 for Range 3). Also, each time you fire remove ammunition from the launcher.

### **Missile Racks**

Missile Racks, Capbuster Missiles or Anti-Cap Torpedoes are one of the most devastating weapons in Stardrive. When you declare that missiles are being fired, place a Missile token on the same hex as the firing ship, with the direction marker facing in the same heading.

Immediately after the next Movement Phase, once all ships have been moved, you may declare for each group of missiles how many hexes it will move and wether it will travel straight, left or right. If a turn is declared then the missiles will turn one hex facing in the middle of their declared movement.

Ships may fire Point Defence turrets at Missile Groups that are adacent to them as if they were fighters. One Missile is destroyed for every 6 rolled.

If, at the end of the gunnery phase a missile is adjacent to an enemy ship, it detonates causing 2d (direct damage) to the target per missile in the group.

### **Beam Cannons**

Cannons are heavier versions of Beam Batteries. Instead of a large rack of emitters, Cannons fire a single powerful beam that spreads damage over a larger area, making the protection offered by Screens ineffective.

B, A and AA Cannons all cause a number of Beam Damage over a fixed range. Cannons do not suffer from falloff penalties, inflicting maximum dice even at their longest range.

Cannons use the special Cannon Arc which is explained in the Basic Rules.

### **Needle Beam**

Needlers as they are often called, are precision weapons designed to take out individual ship systems. Needlers may be fired at targets in the Forward Arc within Range 2. When firing needle beams at a target allocate each one to a system before rolling a D6 for each one. If a needle beam roll results in a 6, the system is disabled and cannot be repaired by Repair Crews (disabled until the end of the current battle)

##  ***Defensive Systems***

### **Screens**

Screen Emitters create a field of disruptive particles around the hull, interfereing with energy based weapons. For each Screen fitted to a ship, the chances of damaging it with beam dice reduce.

With one screen fitted, any 4’s that are rolled cause no damage instead of one.

With two screens fitted, any dice which roll 6 cause a single damage instead of two

With three screens fitted, any dice which roll 5 cause no damage instead of one.

No screens damage per dice = 4/6= 0.66

1 Screen damage per dice = 3/6 = 0.5

2 Screen damage per dice = 2/6 = 0.33

3 Screen damage per dice = 1/6 = 0.15

### **Shield Generator**

During the logistics phase, each Shield Generator allows your ship to recover a single point of Shield to any one facing.

### **Shield Capacitor**

During the design process, yu may distribute 5 shield points between the ships arcs for each Capacitor you fit. This represents the ships maximum Shield Points in that arc.

### **Plated Hull, Heavy Armor, Super Heavy Armor**

Armor is fitted to ships to give them additional toughness at the beginning of each battle. They count as normal Hull Points except they are allocated to a single arc during the Design process.

Each type of armor has a damage reduction as well. Plated Armor only takes a single damage for every two damage it is caused, Heavy Armor only takes a single point of damage for every three damage it is done. Super Heavy Armor is only allowed to be fitted to Capital Ships and can sustain four damage for every point that is fitted.

### **Repair Teams**

During the Logistics Phase, you may allocated each Repair Crew to a system of your choosing. Once allocated roll a D6 for each repair crew on each system. If any of them roll a 6 then the system is repaired until the end of the battle.

Repair Crews count as systems themselves when rolling for Critical Damage, however they cannot be “repaired”

# **Advanced Rules**

## ***Fighters, Point Defence and Area Defence***

Fighters are dynamic weapons that can often swing close battle if used correctly. They must first be launched from a carrier, and then have a limited number of attack runs before they run out of fuel.

Each Hanger holds single fighter, while each Launch tube allows a single fighter to be launched. For Example, Carrier A has 12 Tubes and 36 Hangers, each turn a wing of 12 fighters may be launched until all 36 fighters are in space. Carrier B has 24 Tubes and 24 Hangers, and so it may launch all of its fighters in one turn.

To launch a squadron of fighters, you must place the “Launch!” card with the carriers orders for this turn. Once the ship has finished moving, place a token underneath it to represent the fighter squadron. You may decide when launching fighters how to group them. 12 Fighters can be split into as many as 12 groups of single fighters (although this is not recommended).

Fighters are moved during their own phase. Each squadron can move 12 hexes, and may turn freely during their move. Players take it in turns moving a single squadron each. There are two types of attack a Figther Squadron can make.

Strafing Runs, or Ship Attacks occur when a Squadron moves adjacent to an enemy craft. During the Gunnery Phase the attack is calculated, although fighter losses and damage are not applied until the Logistics Phase. When a squadron makes a Strafing Run, the target ship has an opportunity to fire defensivly. For Each Point defence battery the ship has fitted, and for each Area defence battery within 6 hexes, roll a single dice. Count these as Beam Damage dice, however for each damage caused, a single fighter is hit and must break off its attack. Once defensive fire is finished, the remaining fighters may make their attack run. Treat each fighter as causing a single Beam Damage dice. A Fighter squadron may select which arc it will attack from when dealing damage. During the Logistics Phase, fighters which were hit are removed from the battle, they are either too damaged to continue the fight or were totally destroyed.

Dogfights are attacks that occur between two squadrons of fighters. When calculating dogfights, both players roll Beam Damage Dice equal to the number of fighters in the dogfight. As with a Strafing run, each point of damage takes out a single fighter, however this damage does not stop a fighter from rolling its damage dice (simultaneous damage).

Regardless of the type of attack being made, one a squadron has completed 3 attacks, it must return to the carrier for refuelling and repairs. Remove any remaining fighters from the battlefield immediately after their third attack.

## ***FTL Entry and Reinforcements***

During some scenarios, one or both forces may bring in reinforcements. Only a single ship or squadron may be brought into combat each turn.

When setting up the battlefield, a number of FTL Beacons are placed. Each player takes it in turn placing these on the board. There must be at least 8 hexes between each Beacon.

Before the battle begins both players should secretly select the order in which their reinforcements will arrive. Also selected is the beacon number these reinforcements will arrive at. All this information is kept secret from other opponents.

On the turn that reinforcements start arriving, at the end of the movement phase, the player reveals his first ship, and its expected location. Place the ship on the board in the direction he wishes, then roll 2 dice of different colors to determine the accuracy of the jump. The first dice represents a direction of displacement, and the second the distance displaced. The ship emerges from FTL jump at this point with its original facing. Ships entering via FTL begin with Velocity equal to half their Thrust rating but do not move this turn.

## ***FTL Retreat***

When necessary a ship can engage its FTL drives to make a rapid escape from the battlefield. Such a move is often risky without the proper calculations.

During the orders phase, a player may place the Emergency FTL card next to one of his ships in addition to its normal orders. When the orders are revealed you must declare to all other players that the ship is powering up its FTl drives.

On the following turn, do not place any Orders for this ship. It will move forward half of its current velocity and then enter FTL. If there are no obstacles, ships, missiles or fighters within 6 hexes, the jump is completed without any problems.

If there is ANY obstacle within 6 hexes, roll a single dice and consult this chart.

1: The ship is ripped apart by a gravitational vortex. It is completely destroyed and any ship within 6 hexes takes 1d damage for each 4 mass the FTLing craft had.

2-3: The ship does not enter FTL

4-6: The ship enters FTL perfectly

Ships that have entered the battle using their FTL drives cannot use them to flee the battlefield for 10 turns.

##  ***Boarding Actions***

Initiating a Boarding Action

Manoeuvring a ship to board can be very tricky. The attacking ship must end its movement within 1 Hex of the target, and must not have more than one point of difference between its Velocity/Heading and the Velocity/Heading of the target ship.

If the attacking ship is correctly aligned, during the Gunnery Phase you may board the enemy ship. The boarding ship may not fire any of its weapons during this gunnery phase.

The number of Marine Squads you can attack with is equal to twice the number of marines on board the target ship.

Basic Marines have a boarding value of 2. More advanced troops may become available later.

To calculate the effects of the Boarding Action, both players roll a single D6 and Add the following modifiers.

+1: Less Panic, Higher Assault Value

-1: 5 Panic or more

+1: More than double Assault Value

+1: More than triple assault value

The side with the least score gains one Panic per point of difference between the scores. Also both sides get an opportunity to take out their opponents troops.

Score Winner Looser

Even 4+ 4+

1 difference 3+ 4+

2 difference 3+ 5+

3 difference 2+ 5+

4 difference 2+ 6+

Roll one dice for each Marine Squad. For each dice that rolls higher than the required result, one enemy troop is destroyed.

Panic will drop to zero when the last enemy has been defeated.

#  **Race Design**

Stardrive is designed to accommodate players who wish to design their own play style. Due to balance issues, the original in depth Race/Species creation has been scrapped and replaced with a much simpler system.

## ***Ship Types***

Each player must design a Fleet List detailing 5 ship types that are available to his Alien Race/Species. Whenever a scenario is played with this race, only ships of these types may be selected. Ship designs may be fitted with any combination of systems. The sample races below are from my own Stardrive Galaxy and are each designed to a particular philosophy.

Special Rules

Eventually I would like to play test and balance some bonus and disadvantage combinations to allow more specialised species creation.

### **Sample Race \#1: Humans**

All Human ships fit both Shield and Armor. Armor is placed mainly in the frontal arc giving excellent longevity in a head to head fight. Engines are of middling power, even in Escorts, but with top notch Manuverability. Offensively they fit a combination of Beam and Direct damage, with most ships fitting some sort of ammunition based weaponry.

### **Sample Race \#2: Thi Dane**

Thi Dane ships are designed with extended combat in mind. All of their ships fit high regeneration Shields and fixed frontal mounted energy weapons. Direct damage is unavailable however their ability to damage capital ships in uncompromised because their main weapon ignores the effect of screens. Maximum Thrust ratings is also a must on all Thi Dane designs.

Thi Dane tend to combat larger ships by utilizing a combination of the small Needler ships and the Cruiser sized Troop Carrier. The Needlers disable the drives of any capital ship while the Carrier swoops in to capture it.

### **Sample Race \#3: Sk’Brae Domain**

The Sk’Brae Domain utilizes enormous ships fitted with the hardest armour and the biggest guns. Speed and Manoeuvrability isn’t even considered. During most fire fights their ships hardly move. During smaller skirmishes they rely on Cutters, which are their escort class ship. The same theory applies to these however instead of turret mounted weapons they use Cannons. For defence, Sk’Brae rely on orbital installations and missile Satellites (not to mention their enormous Dreadnoughts).

#  **Scenarios**

## ***Scenario \#1: Patrol***

Two small forces meet in no-mans land. The Mission is simple, gauge the enemies strengths then retreat to friendly territory.

Force \#1: 100 Mass of FTL Capable Escort or Cruiser class ships

Force \#2: 100 Mass of FTL Capable Escort or Cruiser class ships

Setup

Each force is set up along a single table edge. All ships begin on Velocity equal to half their Thrust rating. FTL Disengage is allowed. Do not deploy any obstacles.

## ***Scenario \#2: Escalation***

Now that the enemies weaknesses have been assessed, we can commit a larger force to battle. Once you have engaged the enemy we will send reinforcements.

Force \#1: 50 Mass of FTL Capable Escort or Cruiser class ships

Force \#2: 50 Mass of FTL Capable Escort or Cruiser class ships

Reinforcement \#1: 300 Mass of FTL Capable Ships

Reinforcement \#2: 300 Mass of FTL Capable Ships

Setup

Each force is set up along a single table edge as per Patrol. All ships begin on Velocity equal to half their Thrust rating. FTL Disengage is allowed. Take turns deploying 5 FTL Beacons. Both forces use the same beacons. Reinforcements begin the turn after the first ship is destroyed/flees.

## ***Scenario \#3: Blockade Run***

A small hole in the enemy’s defences has opened up after out last victory. Exploit it to take valuable supplies and reinforcements to a blockaded ground campaign on the designated planet.

Force \#1: 200 Mass of FTL Capable Escort or Cruiser class ships and 100 Mass of Civilian Cargo ships

Force \#2: 100 Mass of FTL Capable ships

Reinforcement \#1: None

Reinforcement \#2: 300 Mass of FTL Capable Ships

Setup

Each force is set up along a single table edge as per Patrol. Attacking ships begin on Velocity equal to half their Thrust rating. Defending ships are stationary. FTL Disengage is allowed only for the Attacking force. Defender deploys 3 FTL Beacons. Reinforcements begin the turn after the first ship is destroyed/flees or the turn after any of the Civilian ships reach the half way point. Civilian ships must exit the Defenders table edge at a Velocity of 2 to make a successful landing.

Civilian Blockade Runner

Mass 20, 20 Hull Points, Thrust 2, Move 1, 2x Point Defence, 1x C Battery. Cannot be formed into squadrons.

##  ***Scenario \#5: Drydock Assault***

A small assault fleet attacks an enemy installation. If the attackers are quick enough they can catch one of the capital ships while its in Drydock.

Force \#1: 200 Mass of FTL Capable Escort or Cruiser class ships

Force \#2: 100 Mass of Defensive Structures or System Defence ships

Reinforcement \#1: None

Reinforcement \#2: 200 Mass of FTL Capable Ships

Setup

The defenders set up their ships in the centre of the battlefield along with all ships that are currently drydocked. Drydocked ships have all systems disabled at the start of the battle, except for their Repair Crews.

**Fighters, Point Defence and Area Defence**
Fighters are dynamic weapons that can often swing a close battle if used correctly. They must first be launched from a carrier, and then have a limited number of attack runs before they run out of fuel.
Each Hanger holds a single fighter, while each Launch tube allows a single fighter to be launched.
*For Example, Carrier A has 12 Tubes and 36 Hangers, each turn a wing of 12 fighters may be launched until all 36 fighters are in space. Carrier B has 24 Tubes and 24 Hangers, and so it may launch all of its fighters in one turn.*
To launch a squadron of fighters, you must place the “Launch!” card with the carriers orders for this turn. Once the ship has finished moving, place a token underneath it to represent the fighter squadron. You may decide when launching fighters how to group them. However the minimum number of fighters in a squadron is 4.12 Fighters can be split into as many as 3 groups of 4 fighters.
Strafing Runs, or Ship Attacks occur when a Squadron moves adjacent to an enemy craft. During the Gunnery Phase the attack is calculated, although fighter losses and damage are not applied until the Logistics Phase. When a squadron makes a Strafing Run, the target ship has an opportunity to fire defensivly. For Each Point defence battery the ship has fitted, and for each Area defence battery within 6 hexes, roll a single dice. Count these as Beam Damage dice, however for each damage caused, a single fighter is hit and destroyed, without the chance to participate in the attack. Once defensive fire is finished, the remaining fighters may make their attack run. Treat each fighter as causing a single Beam Damage dice, remembering to factor in the effects of Screens that are fitted to the target ship. A Fighter squadron may select which arc it will attack from when dealing damage.
I have considered using the system for Boarding actions instead of the regular Dogfight rules so that Dogfights last multiple turns but it may become too complicated during large fleet battles involving many fighters.
Regardless of the type of attack being made, once a squadron has completed 3 attacks, it must return to the carrier for refuelling and repairs. Remove any remaining fighters from the battlefield immediately after their third attack.
