# Factorio as survival game

- Distance from centre controlled how?

- Key buildings limited how

- Shift player activity to exploration and salvaging.

Limiting automation to player position

- Limited supply of battery objects

- Return to centre to recharge

- Player also consumes these to travel

- Player sets up independent outposts and must visit frequently.

- Uptime must be split between each Outpost, since battery objects are limited.

- Ore items have small stack size but smelt to a lot of resources.

Power system

- Nuclear fuel cells as batteries

- Return spent battery after use

- Can only be used near a particular and rare resource (ice geyser)

- Produces heat, which is used to produce electricity.

- Crashed ship can refresh power cells slowly

- Connection radius is zero, so poles do not connect with each other

Structure system

- Special structures have an uncraftable ingredient (power core)

- All structures when mined return their power cores.

- Structures only craftable by hand

- Intermediate items only craftable by machine (or salvaged)

- Hand crafting times very fast

- Intermediate crafting times very slow

- No poles, instead invisible substation placed on special locations.

- No belts, use inserter chains.

- Destroyed buildings with power cores are replaced with wrecks which give power cores when harvested.

Hunger systems

- Player suit consumes power cell very slowly

- Two generators in suit

- Warning if only one generator fuelled

- Player suit has two robots which don't use power.

- Player suit has a roboport and battery

- Player suit has a shield

- If the battery runs out, the player dies

- If within range of a Survival pod, the player battery is topped up regularly.

Elastic Distance system

- Player has a hunger bar which goes down very very slowly when away from the center landing area.

- The further away from the center the player is, the faster it goes down

- Fills up quickly when returning to the center landing area.

Mapping

- Every minute, select a chunk and unchart it

- Damage all player structures in that chunk, if it is already uncharted

- Centre chunk stays charted

- Radars have only one chunk visibility, but much longer range for scanning.

- Findable crashed ents contain beacons, which mean these chunks never dechart.

Exploration

- Early vehicle available

- Fuel efficiency is range

- Vehicles are slow and tough

- Inventory size is important

- Modular upgrades to promote investing in vehicles.

- Main player transport method

- Some areas contain many obstacles, making vehicle transport non viable.

- If a chunk is charted and contains a crashed pod or assembler, then it gets a map markers.

- Radars can scan further than the player can blindly travel.

Infrastructure

- Development comes from creating clear roads between resource rich locations

- The player needs to search for resource deposits near to ice geysers.

- Developing buffer capacity and matching to power buffer is essential to optimizing the players route.

- Each ice geyser can become a town, which can be upgraded to support larger collections of buildings.

- Ice geysers on thier own become recharge points for the player, like waypoints for travel.

- Player roads will most likely be between these points

- Rail is the ultimate infrastructure, but expensive.

Rail

- Stations cost power cores

- Lots of space is used to load and unload trains

- Trains use special fuel which requires a distant Outpost.

- Locomotives also cost power cores

- Wagons carry much less

Building rules

- Cores are needed to create "blue area"

- Cores are needed to build vehicles (so the player wants as few as possible)

- Investing more cores has a "increasing rewards" effect. 2 cores give a x3 effect.

- Structures with more cores require more normal resources.

- Cores are only ever salvaged from map exploration.

- The size of a collection of buildings is always ultimately limited by how many cores are invested at that location

- Automation costs cores.

# Changing how death works in the NPE/Campaign

Why

Currently death is very abrupt. The character loses their last hitpoint and the game immediately displays the Game Over screen. During testing we saw players immediately click the Restart button, even when there was an autosave from moment before.

This abrupt warning gives the impression that it is very serious that the player died when really, in Factorio, death is fairly meaningless.

Instead I propose a softer death. The player sees the character dead on the ground, and we fade to black. The player is respawned without a warning to let them know it was not such a big deal to die.

How \#1

- Fade to Black

- Fade in to show the factory still running, at increased speed

- Fade to black

- Fade in, show player awake at spawn point, Compi nearby

- Compi provides exposition

Questions

- How much does Compi explain?

- Do we even say if the player died, or let them assume.

- Is this a new body or the same one?

- Should the player find their corpse?

- Should the player lose their items? If so, all of them?

- Will it confuse the player if no explanation is given?

Problems

- If the cutscene is too long, and unskippable, players will become annoyed.

- If the player does not lose enough items, then death becomes a teleporter

- This becomes a quick way to time warp

How \#2

- Do not display the game over screen

- Go directly to the respawn dialog (as happens in Multiplayer)

- Player respawns with Compi next to them to give exposition

Questions

- No time has passed, what is the cost to the player?

Problems

- Is this too abrupt? We do not teleport the player in any other situations.
