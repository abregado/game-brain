### Concept: Massivly Single Player “balance market”

Points costs for a single (or even multiplayer) game are dynamic. As players use them, the price for every one else goes up. Items that players do not use drop in price until they are low enough that players consider them worth taking.

These prices are updated each time the player starts the game. No login information is required.

The aim here is that single player balance is achieved by monitoring the actions of players. Also all content will eventually get used as it becomes cheaper.

This was trialed in Counterstrike once and failed. The glock became so cheap that people could buy thousands of them, lagging out servers across the world.

### Concept - Proceedurally generated creatures DEUX

Instead of randomly generating the creatures in a rougelike dungeon, utilize a system where creatures interact and fight eachother, with the winners populating the dungeon level. Some creatures have non-conflicting goals so can live in harmony. Most compete during the generation algorithims for space. Creatures can also have “genetic arms races” and get better if they compete and survive to regrow their populations. These second generation creatures could be better and eventually move back into an area.

### Concept - Xcom Time Units + Speed

The original Xcom used time units to determine how many actions a unit could take. Weapons had a variety of fire modes which used a % of the weilders total TU. This meant that units with more TU could move further but still shoot the same number of times.

The proposed change is to have both Speed and Agility Units. These combine to give the total TU for the trooper. Weapon fire modes only use a % of the Agility Units. Now you can have units that are fast, but take a long time to fire, as well as units that are very fast to fire but are a bit slower.

### Concept - Xcom weight penalties

As you equip a soldier with more stuff, his encumberance increases. This currently effects the rate of stamina and energy decay/regeneration. In addition, encumberance should increase the % cost in TU of performing any action.

### Concept - Instagib + Skill

An instagib gamemode in which players hitting eachother cause Heat to build up in their target. The weapons fire slowly like an instagib rifle. A player can only be hit three times consecutivly before exploding from the heat. If a player avoids being hit for 5 second, one point of heat is dissipated. The rifle fires once every 2 second, and if a target is hit, the countdown is reset.

### Concept - Hitscan and projectile instagib rifle

Replace the instagib rifle with another weapon. Primary fire shoots a triple burst of fast moving, but not hitscan rounds. Secondary fire is a hitscan beam, but has a slower rate of fire.

### Concept - No trigger holding

Holding down the trigger doesnt refire the weapon when its ready. The player must release and depress the button again to fire. Weapon models should have a ready state to show when they can refire.

### Concept - Firing Delays

All weapons have a slight delay before firing, but are more accurate than standard arcade FPS firearms. Players will always have to compensate for target speed more.

### Concept - Charged movements

Players do not jump, dodge and crouch by pressing a button. Instead the button must be held down and released to activate. Holding longer before releasing increases effect, but holding for too long causes a negative effect. Doing this with the jump key allows the player to launch his avatar towards the crosshair. This could be context sensitive based on the angle of view, determining if the character leaps forward or straight up.

Standing on a jump pad does nothing unless the player presses jump. Then his jump is amplified.

### Concept - Active Defences

Players can deflect incoming attacks by pressing a button just in time. When doing this they cannot fire for a short period.

### Concept - Damage determines view distance

When a player has taken some damage, others cannot see them from so far away. Once a player is almost dead, his avatar is visible only to characters at a very close range.

This could be invisiblity or something else to help the player blend in. Perhaps color change to the environment, partial transparency or removal of player pips. Maybe when on full health the player emits light, and it dims as they take damage.

### Concept - Dual stage reloading

Each weapon can be fired very few times before reloading. Each gun only has a few clips. Once out of clips, there is a much longer reloading time before you can get extra clips back. Standing still can increase the speed of the second stage reload. This might promote players switching out to other, less suitable, weapons instead of reloading.

### Concept - After firing, a player can be tracked

Whenever a player fires, he builds up points. These points tick down over time. While there are points remaining, the players movement are shown to other players. The more points, the longer the trail behind the player.

2020: highlight bodymesh so others can guess distance.

### Concept - Minimap abstract

Each map has a simple minimap, showing the rough division of the map into 4-5 zones. When players have fired recently, or are on full health, other players can see that there is a player in the same zone.

### Concept - Players explode when they die

When a player reaches zero life, their corpse explodes shortly after they die.

### Concept - Change weapon on death

Each player spawns equipped with a single weapon. When the player dies, he is presented with a new weapon to use until death. The player is never given the same weapon twice in a row. Perhaps each weapon can only be used once, but the player can choose the order. In this way, weapons become the number of respawns available to each player.

2020: match weapons with passive bonuses that are active regardless of current weapon.

### Concept: Multiplayer Notepad wiki page organisation instead of document style

After a discussion about “what is 3d?” I thought about making a 2d maps with “portals” to other 2d maps to create a 3d space.

The analogy would be to compare a regular document with a wiki. Documents have pages (areas) collected in a linear fashion. Wikis have pages as well, but they are connected in a non-linear fashion.

Perhaps a game world could be fashioned in the same way. The player can move about in their game world freely. When they find a “portal” they can “jump to the top of a new page”. In this case the centre of the new map (0,0) would be the “top”. The only other navigation option is to go “back”. This brings them back to the portal on the previous world. Not all maps would be linked to eachother, so this creates obstacles in the “3rd dimension”

### Concept: Multiplayer Notrepad Abstract No pathfinding worker NPCs

The player can create NPCs which move about under their own intuition. They can do “work” in the game world, and even know which objects they can work on, but will not pathfind between them. Instead they move in very predictable ways that the player must utilize.

The most basic example would be a pusher. This NPC pushes red and blue blocks, but if it encounters anything else it reverses its direction. If this happens while pushing a block, it will reverse direction, leaving the block behind. Add a puller and suddenly you have a way to extract and move blocks in the game world.

Players will be able to place “control blocks” or just some limited types in order to control their workers.

Creating optimal shapes must increase the power of the player somehow.

If we provide the player with enough types of worker, and a goal, we should see complex worker/block systems emerge from the playerbase.

Competitively there will be optimal ways to interrupt an opponent or even subvert his system for your own goals.

OPTIONS: NPCs can also be stationary and effect objects within a radius or line of sight. Maybe there is a “black hole” which draws in nearby tiles, or a “shover” that throws them along straight line. Other operators of programming could be “organiked”, or created from simple mechanisms.

### Concept: Volatile areas in a MPNP environment

Certain “structures” will generate an effect on the page they are on. These are in the form of placement rules. (See: Game of Life)

These could be as simple as shapes utilizing the same tile, or complex designs.

Once operational, the whole page is effected by a rule. Example:

If there is an INORGANIC ‘x’ within 10 tiles of another ‘x’ they will move towards each other. The number of ‘x’s that are adjacent to each other multiply this effect by their number. This would cause all the ‘x’s on the page to begin moving towards one another, slowly building in power until the effect is drawing them into a tight ball.

Another effect might see the formation of 3x3 ‘x’s cause an explosion, creating a cloud of random ‘b’s. Suddenly all of the ‘x’s on the map are in danger of being destroyed instead of collecting! If ‘b’s are of lower value than ‘x’s then this could be used as a type of attack and the defensive player should work towards removing the offensive effect causing structure.

Combinations of these could see whole pages being exploited for a particular use. Also some structures or NPC combinations may only work on certain pages.

When these structures are found in the wild pages, players could screenshot them to share the designs with others. This could create some interesting meta gaming.

OPTION: The players cannot see active effects listed on the GUI. They must observe the game world to discover what is active.

OPTION: Viewing a structure for long enough will reveal its secrets and its design can be replicated more easily.

OPTION: Structures only affect an area around them, not the whole page. This could cause problems at the boundaries and with tracking effects on a per tile basis.

OPTION: Some structures could modify game parameters such as:

- Player vision eg//Coloration of tiles

- Tick Speed

- Ability of players to move their viewport

- Ability of players to place tiles

- Ability for certain other structures to form

- Breaking a set game rule

### Concept: It is easier to build near to resources (encouraging opportunity)

“Resources” can spawn in the game world. They can be harvested and then structures built using them. However it is much cheaper to build structures near to the resources.

If a structure is built on top of resources it needs, these are used for the build costs. Any building costs not met by resources underneath are multiplied by a number. This number creates the indirect cost of logistics in the game world.

Gameplay Example// The player needs to build more housing. These come in the form of wooden huts. The player sends workers out to cut down some wood, and then to bring the logs back to cut into planks. A hut might need 100 planks and a log produces 10 planks. If the player builds the hut on top(or next to) the log where it fell, the log might be worth 50 planks. If he fells two trees next to each other he might even get a greater discount and end up with a larger house.

This mechanic would be used to encourage players to build in places of opportunity, rather than using cookie cutter solutions. It also encourages players to develop opportunity recognition skills, rather than just reading a forum/wiki whenever they get stuck.

### Concept: Players generate mess

As in real life, doing work generates mess. This can be harmless mess, or it can affect you and others negatively. Perhaps your mess is of value to someone else.

Waste products as an abstract idea could work in a game also. As players work in the game world, they generate a hindering mess which affects future work. They can simply ignore it, or move elsewhere when it gets bad... or clean up after themselves.

In a team play game, those players who do not clean up may find their team becomes angry at the fact.

In competitive play, perhaps leaving mess could be a valid strategy.

### Idea: Slow mech game. 

Mechwarrior was great and everyone remembers it being great, however it was very much circle strafing, turret aiming, zero strategy gameplay. The terrains were mostly open, you had full information about your opponent, weapon ranges and damages were not really considered relative to one another (pack on the best Damage/Weight weapons). Also leg shooting was so predominant as to be vomit worthy.

Position on the battlefield needs to be more important. Teamplay with other mechs in your squad also need to be more important.

Mechwarrior games could benifit from squad level tactics. Perhaps an order system similar to Republic Commando/SWAT4 could work. You Lay out the overall strategy and your AI attempts to stick to it.

To combat the circle strafing mechanic, Mechs could have less actual agility, perhaps requiring to be stationary before firing, or they could have a higher size:agility gap. This would see the smaller mechs much faster than the larger ones.

### Feature: Limited Bandwidth optical nerves for interesting viewmode combinations

The player has access to a sophisticated suite of viewmodes that replace his eyes. Optical nerves leading to the brain have been enhanced but there is still limited bandwidth with which to transfer the information to the brain (not to mention the speed at which the brain can decipher this information).

The character has visual spectrum, IR, UV, Nightvision, Brightvision(polarizing), Synapse detection, Movement, Radar and Sonar as well as some way to distinguish between organic and inorganic compounds.

Each of these has a bandwidth cost based on the current FOV and resolution of scan. They can be activated together or separately, but as there is limited bandwidth, they are much more effective (higher vision arc, higher resolution or range) alone or in small combinations. Turning them all on will reduce the player to staring through a tunnel of grainy imagery.

Different set ups can be saved and quickly changed between. However, similar to flash blindness, the characters implants can only handle a set rate of change. Increasing the information input by jacking the resolution or turning on a lot of modes may cause a form of “intel blindness”.

Threats can be cloaked from some or all of these sensors. Also various effects could blind or jam the players use of some of the modes.

### Narrative: Excuse for solo exploration on foot of an alien world

Earth has found a way to transport very small amounts of material across the interstellar void almost instantly. Explorers are teleported to the planet inside a drop pod. Since the energy cost is extremely high, everything is useful or can be dismantled for survival. No excess is allowed for.

Also the exit point is not very accurate, so the drop is generally dropped in orbit. The pod then releases a small satellite which moves into orbit and can send FTL messages back to earth. The pod then descends to the surface.

Of course this doesn't always go to plan. The pod may exit inside the atmosphere, meaning there is no time to drop the communication satellite. Sometimes the pod exits too far from a planet and so must expend its limited fuel simply entering orbit to be retrieved. A worse death is of course to end up in a situation where the pod cannot land correctly, killing its occupant.

If everything goes to plan, the satellite will relay information with earth and after the mission time is complete, the pod can be transported back to earth. The return journey is much more accurate but also suffers from relativistic issues, the successful explorer arriving home only after several hundred years. Alternatively, if the planet is found to be useful for colonization, the explorer may enter statis planetside and await settlers, and the eventual fixed portal home.
