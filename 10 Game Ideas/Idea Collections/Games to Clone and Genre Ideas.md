## Platformer Ideas

- Simple Jump/Shoot/Duck Platformer with Scaling Difficulty and Time Pressure

- Tower Defence Platformer. Waves of creeps attack and your character must stop them from advancing through the level. Waves increase in power each time.

- Explore a alien derilict orbiting earth. Abstract Zero G movement. Can only explore limited amounts of the world with each trip. A Dig Deeper style game. Doors to deeper sections must be opened with timed switches. Objects can be installed to do work during subsequent forays.

- Grappling Hook Platformer. The opposite of the Zero G concept. Keep moving up exploring the game world. Climb higher and higher collecting score. The higher you go the more health you lose when you fall. Player must search for supplies on his way up else risk failing.

- Platformer on a space station with Rotating life support sections. The levels wrap around onto themselves in one direction. Other parts can be entered and the axis of travel change.

- Alien Breed with sticky cover. 2d. Certain locations on the map have bonuses to cover. Real shootout feel of trading fire. Suppressive fire actually makes walls that block movement.

- Survival platformer.

## Adventure Ideas

- Tree diagram map to be explored. Different nodes have different styles of movement (top down, platformer, click to go). Side shooter for travelling between nodes.

# Economy Game

- raw materials are generated from nothing, but require consumable components

- processed materials are made from raws, needs infrastructure

- components are made from processed materials and cant be reversed.

- equipment-infrastructre is made from components snd processed materials and can be recycled at a high cost. most components are recovered, but much of the processed is lost.

- components are also used as consumables during repair and for ongoing maintenance costs.

Processes lower in the chain requires more consumables, so that demand is driven by activity. Miners need drills, which demands components which demands infrasructure which consumes components, which needs processed materials, which needs raw material which needs miners.

each activity should produce the prerequisites for one component, but most components should need two or more activities.

to begin with all processes are controlled by a 'bank player'.

if activity drops, so does demand

as activity drops, consumable costs will drop making activity cheaper.

rate of expansion is also based on activity, since everything has a consumable upkeep.

# Sov Mechanics

aims

- to give players a reason to stay in one place

- make asset stockpiling important

- give value to space the longer you hold it

- having neighbours reduces value of space

- having activity creates content for others

- options for disruption

- advantage to defenders against conquer

- advantage attackers when disrupting.

- defenders can lock systems which are surounded by thier own systems.

- reason to attack but not conquer

- reasons why players will want to remove outposts

- reasons to leave space empty.

So you sould want to hold your space, but also have as few other systems nearby that are taken. Ideas:

- the more infrastructure in a constellation, the less efficient it is overall

- having one system should be better than all, if the others are empty.

- holding more space is for tactical reasons, rather than profit reasons.

- sites last much longer, and good ones are rare.

- quality of space increases per site despawn but resets if the scanning stations are attacked.

- owning both sides of a gate means you cn restrict access.

- choose what a system is for, defense or profit

- systems are stronger per empty system around them

- place stronghold systems to protect others

- disrupting a system means it doesnt count as adjacent for 24 hours.

- unmaintained space reverts after a month.

- setting up takes time and can be interuppted with disruption

- fight to make it unprofitable to stay, not to annihilate

- tax gates you control
