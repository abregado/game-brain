**Bigger Numbers game**

Well, most games are about getting bigger numbers.The player in this case has a few basic mechanics to deal with.

Warm up time - every action takes time before it begins working

Limited Space - every item takes up some space, and the amount of space is limited.

Pulsing logistics - Logistics are not smooth, inputs come in very large pulses which are near impossible to smooth out

Thematically, this might be shipments arriving to be processed. No matter how big a player's set of gameplay tools gets, the situation is always most dire immediately after getting more resources. Perhaps it could be people transported to a new and undeveloped world.

Processes going on while the player is engaged in something else is really important. The player should always be worried about what is happening with his creation elsewhere.

Limited Information is also important. The player should only be able to view one part of the game world at a time.

Very high cost to transfer game objects between different parts of the world might help to perpetuate the “high impulse” logistical gameplay I am seeking.

Space should increase in cost exponentially as it is claimed until it is not possible to get more. Also, more space should require more input.

A 1d gameworld would make the mechanics much easier to focus on. Locations are displayed on a 2d plane, but are actually not linked via this plane. Each has a number of connections to other locations, but adjacency is not calculated by visual position.

The setting could be a continuously growing colony, displayed side on. Costs for clearing land and building foundations goes up as you expand sideways. Material and technology costs increase as you build higher, or deeper.

Expansion “slots” could be pre-generated randomly, so each playthough would be different. Unlocking a slot/location would not always give the player exactly the location they want. There can be different types, and should be penalties for NOT using them (they drain resources even when empty?)

No technology tree. Instead, players can request limited supplies of unconstructable material. These are delivered if certain goals are reached.

Constant pressure. The play experience should become more and more intense as time passes. Difficulty is always increasing, and it also jumps at fixed points.

Changing situations. The game environment should slowly move between a number of states (or environmental seasons) in which different styles of gameplay are more valuable.

Lose-Lose choices. Allow other colonies to be built nearby? Then you can shuttle more and more colonists away to ease the burden on your life support. But if the other colonies grow faster than you then they will have power in the local markets!

The cost for upgrades should be a significant portion of the total storage space, to encourage smoothing out decisions. Construction should also take considerable amounts of time. Perhaps the player will change his mind half way through a long project.

Locations are chunks of a base. These contain slots of different size and type. New slots are generated by creating new locations. New locations are generated randomly and may not be what the player wants. The player can spend more to get more location options.

To change a location, the whole location must be disabled for the entirety of the construction process. Each location has multiple slots, so not filling them all at the same time can be very costly.

Some locations have special uses. An example would be an area of flat terrain could be used for cheap solar power, or a wind farm, or a greenhouse. But only one. If the player builds a colony structure there, the other options are no longer available.

Locations can also be regenerated to contain more slots. This requires the entire location to be removed, and although its location will stay the same, it will be regenerated. Early game might be about accessing the cheapest locations very quickly, and then replacing locations with “better” ones.

The special traits can be added to Locations during the construction phase, but should always come at the cost of removing another ability. Adding a glass roof might allow plants to be grown in the location, but it will remove the ability to build new locations on top of it.

To slow expansion down arbitrarily, The player will need to invest some resources in “Design”. This takes time and after it is done, the player is presented with a number of possible expansion options. The player can only select one, or none. The cost and time element means the player must take advantage of what is offered. Each option will have its own costs and time investment, to be weighed up against the cost of just re-designing.

For now lets stick to the theme of running a new space colony

Immigrants come in LARGE waves. You need to cater for them, AND integrate them into your space colony. Immigrants have little to no useful skill when they arrive and must be trained, fed, oxygenated and housed.

Services you need to provide for your colonists to keep them alive

- Life Support

- Food

- Living Space/Healthcare/Entertainment - Services

While you turn Immigrants into Workers by training them. Untrained Immigrants cant staff many of the colony services, but there are also jobs that need unskilled labor. If trained workers need to complete unskilled work, they become less efficient at all jobs, and actually consume more Life Support and Food (needing more entertainment and being angry more often)

Major Mechanic is keeping trained workers in skilled roles and managing the levels of unskilled/skilled labor.

These levels are maintained by building Machines in your slots. The first machines are:

- Oxygen Recycler - Produces lots of Life Support

- Hydroponics Lab - Produces lots of Food

- Habitat - Provides lots of housing, but uses power, leaks Life Support. Needs more maintenance.

Structures require the following to operate at peak efficiency

- Solar Farms - generates Power

- Workshop - provides maintenance

Each slot has a Size attribute. This affects how much of its product it provides. Since slot sizes are randomized, players cannot create cookie cutter colonies.

### Colony One

A remake of a small game I created when I was 12, but with expandability!

Game Components

- Bases

  - Resources in base

  - Stats in base

  - Modules in Base

- Resources

  - Identified by name

  - have a category

  - have a quantity

- Modules

  - Have a unique identifier

  - Have a type

  - Have a priority

  - Each has a series of stat prerequesites

  - Each has a process

  - Process is run each tick if stat prerequesites are available, in order of priority

  - Modules with the same priority run at the same time

  - can provide stats if process was successful

  - Have a minimum offline timer (if they go offline, they are unavailable for a minimum number of turns, also works as a construction time)

- Processes

  - Take resources

  - Make Resources

  - Have a cooldown period

  - Have a processesing time

Game Logic

- Game Loop

  - Sort modules by priority

  - Set modules active based on last turns stats

  - Run processes for active modules

    - Add/subtract resources as you go

  - Calculate stats for next round

Stats (each has max and used)

- Labour and Housing (current and max stat)

- Energy Production and Infrastructure (current and max)

- Current Cargo and Cargospace (most modules provide a small amount, dedicated ones Lots)

- Industrial Efficency (increases Cooldown period, based on free cargospace)

Gameplay

- Player can change things in their colony

- the game does not run game loops while the game is running

- the player quits the application

  - Base is saved locally

  - Time of close is saved locally

- the player opens the game

  - Base is loaded

  - current time is checked

- The game loop plays one loop for every 10 minutes since the application was closed

- could skew the time here. One tick for every 10 minutes after the first hour.

- the player can move a slider to see how the colony advanced over this time

User Interface

- on open use “playback gamestate”

  - slider to view the game at any given timestep

  - output only, no modification

  - option to finish playback

  - particle juice to show increase/decrease in resources

  - summary at end showing difference in stocks

- input mode

  - module overview (fullscreen module list)

    - sort by type/online/priority

    - activate/deactivate modules

    - change priority

    - group by type

  - cargo overview (fullscreen cargo manifest)

    - dump cargo option

    - sort my category

    - show expected increase/decrease

    - mark for trade

  - Base overview (full screen view of base)

    - shows minimal cargo and module info

    - option to build new modules

    - option to remove modules (with minimal refund)

    - show show many module slots are still available

  - Trade window

    - shows time until next ship arrival

    - shows involuntary trades

    - shows sale contracts available (accepting earlier means better rates)

    - shows items available

    - shows balance of trade (difference is lost)

Tech Levels

First tech level should include modules that have low offline times but long cooldowns. Each will generate a host of resources, but create a very small number of them. The bottleneck here should be labor. Structures that generate Labor should also produce housing of the same value, and require an item called Cryopods to build. This item is only available from the Cargo deliveries, and will come at a fixed pace.

Cargo space should be plentiful, and modules in this tech level should not require very much power.

Only modules that produce resources should require stats (labor/energy). These can be built and then controlled by using the priorities system.

Every module in this tech level requires items from the trade ships to build, as well as local resources. Resources that are requested by the trade ships should be different than those needed to build modules, so the player must decide where to focus efforts.

The second tech level should see maintaining Energy levels as the primary concern. New modules that create more energy than be used are available, as well as those that can convert energy into Items:Batteries. These energy creating modules consume resources to continue producing energy, unlike tech 1 modules. The game is now about maintaining smooth supply lines of cargo to keep energy levels up. This also requires matching cooldown/offline times and processing amounts.
