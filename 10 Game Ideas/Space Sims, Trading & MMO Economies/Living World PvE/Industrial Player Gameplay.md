Industrial Players control large multi-part craft which drop a multitude of smaller objects. There is no minute-minute gameplay, but instead gameplay revolves around refreshing information and planning how you will re-arrange the larger parts.

Setup and Packup times are very long, so wanting to play for longer sessions is encouraged.

Every item has packup/setup times, so that there are soft borders to when a placement becomes “unprofitable”.

Each Industrial Craft drops 1-6 smaller craft which are actually deployable structures. One of these is a Hauler vehicles which moves the other immobile objects. Players can take more Haulers, which means more mobility and reduced setup/packup times, but less overall effectiveness. These craft are still several times larger than Combat Craft.

Resources are moved between these Larger structures and the “Main Craft” by very small craft (transfer craft). These are smaller than Combat Craft and very fragile. The Industrial player does not however control these directly, they are distributed by the AI. The Player only selects links between module inputs and outputs, and allocates a quantity of these craft.

One of the Structures is a large cargo container which can be Hauled back to the “Main Craft”. On a per-item basis this is faster than when the items are hauled with transfer craft, as all transfer craft have a pickup/setdown time as well.

Main Craft have a limited number of different Transfer Craft as well. To begin with there are more than you can usefully field, and they are cheap to replace once back at base. They are very easy to be destroyed, and therefore represent both the “health points” of your Industrial Craft as well as the “efficiency” of your operation over all. Once enough of them are destroyed, the player needs to pack up and head home, or pay for another player to bring them more supplies.

While the player waits for the next move they can be planning moves even further along, making fine adjustments and updating their information on the best resource locations.

In addition to planning the placement of buildings, information gathering activities and resource movement, the Industrial Player must also lay down “Bounty zones”, “Patrol Zones” and “Defence Zones”. These reward Combat players for completing actions within in the zones. Bounty Zones reward players with credits for destroying enemies inside the zone. Patrol Zones reward all nearby combat players when they are touched, and respawn on a fixed timer. Defence Zones just give credits over time to a limited number of combat players (just for staying in the zone). The rewards for these are set by the Industrial Player.

Industrial Players also need to care for the resource needs and repairs of Combat players in the area by laying down structures that can refuel, rearm and repair. These also have limited resources and must be serviced either by Cargo Containers, Haulers or Transfer craft. The prices for these services are also set by the Industrial player. If they do not offer favorable rates, the Combat Players will probably leave.

Over time the rate of resource extraction on a Drill will drop and eventually it will need to be moved to a new location. While Structures are being moved they are more vulnerable and will be actively targeted enemy units. Bounty and Defence zones will probably be updated during a redeployment so that it is protected by Combat Players.

When a Industrial Main Craft is stationary, all of its power is diverted to running its Industrial systems and repair systems. While moving, these craft have powerful point defence weapons that can protect them from sustained attack, as long as supplies hold out. It can take a long time to get a Main Craft into position and ammunition is expensive, so it is probably cheaper to hire Mercenary combat players to escort you to the resource field.
