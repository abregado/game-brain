A deconstruction and rebuild of

[*http://www.nuclearpowersimulator.com/*](http://www.nuclearpowersimulator.com/)

to be played on PC and Android with more strategy feel

### Phase 1

Player is managing space ships docking with a refuelling station

To visualize output energy, blocks of energy should be shown. Incoming goal ships have an amount of blocks to fill and a maximum charge rate.

Player has a number of docking ports with different amounts of Stored Energy, maximum Transfer Rate and ship size. Each port also has an energy generation rate

Interface is simple, docking ports are shown at the bottom of the screen, with their statistics displayed next to them. Ships float in space showing their stats and needs. Also a timer is shown next to each ship which displays how long it will wait without getting docking permission before it leaves.

Once a ship has docked, it will be recharged based on the smaller of the two maximum transfer rates (ship and dock). Ships also have a maximum time they will stay docked before leaving.

Ships are randomly generated. If a ship is generated that cannot dock with your ports then, instead of being added to the play area, it is tabulated for the later report phase. Each ship, on generation also calculates the fastest it can be refuelled using your best port. If this number is longer than its “Patience” timer then it is also tabulated.

Score/Money is paid when a ship undocks. If it is full then you get the full price per unit filled, otherwise you get the penalty rate (not to mention the docking port was taken up for a long time)

Every 3-5 minutes, ships will no longer request to dock, and once the last ship has left port, the player gets a report saying how many ships passed by without docking, and why, a profit report and and an option to upgrade one docking port to one of three randomly generated port models. The player can, at this point, see all the stats for his ports, as well as all the stats for the new models he was offered. Only one port can be chosen and it replaces one of his existing ports.

Gameplay should be quite frantic to begin with. Ships should have low requirements so that the player has more interactions per minute. The report screen is to get the player used to start stop gameplay which will be introduced in later development phases.

### Phase 2

Controls integrated into the objects. Click and hold then drag to set the levels. Release over the on/off area to disable the machine.

Baseline demand fluctuates slowly

Peak Power spikes are generated by spaceships docking and requiring recharge

While machine is being repaired or maintained it runs at half its current capacity. To be replaced the whole system needs to be turned off.

All machines break down slowly, and faster when worked at their maximum. Time since last maintenace check also effects this. Players should want to maintain their machines as little as possible while still retaining functionality.

The time used to replace/repair them is the same no matter how damaged they are.

Most of the replacement cost comes from needing to disable the unit/run it at lower capacity while it is being repaired.

If there is a breakdown then the system must be shut down and the part gets replaced.

Machines build up to the set level. This is gradual. Nothing changes instantly. The system should reach an equilibrium eventually, but be in a constant state of decay.

The reactor screen should be separate to the docking screen. As well as controls for each machine, the player can also set priority for each docking port. Higher priority ports will receive power first if the total output drops below a certain level.

The player taps and holds on a machine to bring up its options. Main options are operation level, and replace/maintain. The player can only have two machines being maintained or replaced at a time. Replacement takes a lot longer than Maintenance.

The rate of new ships arriving should be slowed down at this phase, so that the player can focus on both screens. The speed at which the reactor equalizes should be very slow so that the player can move back to the docking screen for 30-60 seconds without worry that the whole thing is going to explode.

The overall intensity of play should be lower now, so increase the length of each round to 5-8 minutes.

New options to increase the amount of heat dissipation, antimatter rods and solar panels (baseline power). Player gets one upgrade for this screen per round, in the same style as the docking upgrade (randomly generated part to replace existing part).

### Phase 3

Machines can be placed where you like into a 4x4 (or larger) grid

connections must be made correctly between neighbouring machines, where inputs/outputs are shared.

The player can build multiple of each machine for redundancy, or to increase overall output.
