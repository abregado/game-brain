ELITE Space fighter simulation ideas written after playing Vegastrike, Darkstar One, X3, Privateer, Original Elite games.

Fixes required

**Early game: Tutorial mixed with campaign start to teach basic mechanics**

Tutorials should NEVER involved a player sitting still listening to a lecture. Due to the complex nature of neutonian physics, most clones have INCREDIBLY tedius lecture tutorials. These can take up to an hour to complete.

The first missions of games in this genre also tend to be repetative, if only becasue the player is doomed to fail the first few attempts. No effort is spared to ease the player from tutorial to campaign play. If a player continues to play after being destroyed almost instantly (each time going through the tedious travel routine) then he might stand a chance of finishing the game before ragequitting. Most likely not.

The tutorial and first missions should be amalgamated into one set of fixed storyline missions. There should be a high mitigation of risk and loss during these missions. The player should never get a GAME OVER simply for wanting to play.

I would have the first mission see the player thrown into an escalating firefight involving many craft. The longer the player holds out for, the harder the campaign difficulty will be set to. Regardless of how the player dies, he will be rescued and the campaign will begin. Perhaps the area this enormous battle took place could be a debris field that plays a primary role durng the campaign.

**Mission tedium: how to combine gameplay elements to make for a more enjoyable mission**

This is always a killer in ELITE games. The missions are single part and tedious, taking place in deep space. To reduce this tedium, while also increasing the number of available options, I feel a combination of Location, Mission, Adversary and Time Limit should be used.

Locations should always be near to a landmark feature. Asteroids, Low Orbits, near to large structures or moons are all good and well but the player will expect all of these.

Internal locations will also work. Perhaps here are hollow superstructures left from a previous age or alien race. Perhaps you can fly through mining tunnels carved into an asteroid. Debris fields or ancient battlegrounds(salvage fields) could also make for both obstables and a source of conflict.

Missions tend to be KIll X or “Fetch my space slippers”. Thats fine for most situations. One tactic is to mask this from the player with more and more complexity. I beleive when the player is in one of these situations, it should be very clear to them what they have to do. However more criteria can be added. Kill X inside Y area, Fetch slippers while avoiding detection.

Missions should not generate player activity. Instead the opposite should hold true. Players should be encouraged to participate in some passive, low risk gameplay and during such gameplay, missions become available to increase the risk and reward. This also means that thre are gameplay options nearby to the player and stops needless long distance travel (another tedious component of the genre).

**Starting strength: making the player competativly equipped at the early game**

Elite games have a tendancy to start the player off well below the level of the easiest NPCs. My only guess is so that the upper level cap is not reached as quickly. That said, if lowering the stating player to this frustrating point is to squeeze a few hours of extra time from the “play hours” of a game, I will be very annoyed!

Players should be able to be involved in epic battles from the first 5 minutes of play. Nobody wants to spend their first 3 hours fighting against a single NPC that they have a 30% chance to defeat.

**High ceilings: constantly show the player there is something larger he could be getting into**

The pilot should constantly be encountering the next steps in various advancement chains. This is like window shopping for upgrades. First you encounter more than one option for upgrade, experience how it effects the environemtn and then when you have made up your mind, upgrade to it.

The enemies you encounter as well as the friendly NPCS should be outfitted with tech similar to your own (but always a little higher).

Rarely the player should encounter NPCS with vastly higher level than is available. It should be mentioned to the player that these NPCs are well outside the players league. Perhaps hints can be dropped as to the fastest/most efficent way to pursue items seen equipped to these NPCs.

Another option could see the player being equipped with a new piece of equipment ‘on loan’ for a single mission.

**Taming the void: Space is a big place. How do we create interesting landmarks?**

Most of space is empty. Combat and interesting situations will never begin/take place in deep space. Every event should take place nearby to a landmark, not only to provide perspective, but to give the player a sense of location to remember the fight with.

Not all locations should be available from any other. Planets often block a flight path when a ship is in near orbit. The player should be encouraged to choose a different location to explore for a while, or to set up waypoints between common locations.

**Simplified controls: ELITE clones are notorious for their 12 axis, 15 finger controls**

The player must be empowered when dealing with noncombat controls so that he can concentrate fully on combat manuveres. One idea would be to use two control methods that are toggleable. Combat and Non-combat flight modes could rebind all important controls.

When outside of combat, launch missile hardly needs to be bound to the right mouse button. Also primary weapons shouldnt be on left mouse button as you could accidently fire upon a friendly or starbase. When in non-combat mode, flight should be more precise, and systems such as tractor beams, scanners, communication, navigation, autopilot and manuevering thusters should be bound to the main keys.

In combat the pilot should be able to concentrate on flying on a knifeedge. If a button is requried to fight enemies then it should be easily accessable. Primary buttons should be in a place where the player can hover fingers over them and secondary buttons should never require the player to remove these hovering fingers away.

**Joymouse: how to combine these things into a coherent control system**

Currently most spacesims are flown using mouse+keyboard or joystick and keyboard. Even then, mouse OR joystick do not have enough axis for a fully 3d space flight sim. A new control model must be developed that allows movement in 3d.

**Trade routes: redesign or remove from game? Automate or Intergrate?**

Moving goods from one place to another and back again is a staple for the ELITE clone. However it is always boring, tedius and very very time consuming. Most times it is also required that you grind this task for a few hours before you can outfit a decent combat ship.

One option is to remove them completly. However I prefer the idea of automating these tasks. Players rent NPC cargo pilots to move items for them. Supply and Demand is represented by production per minute (with pre-requisite ingredients hvaing local demand per minute), instead of the buy low/sell high philosophy.

This could also generate missions for the player. Their own or other NPC cargo craft can come under attack. Survivablility would have to be high enough that the player can arrive to help before destruction takes place. Alternativly the player could take control of some local defence craft via remote control to fight off the attackers.

**GUI representation of local space, danger, signature and likleyness of nearby stealth craft.**

Something I have never seen in a space game. Your radar/sensors show craft you can see nearby. However stealth in the void would be an amazing tactic. The player needs to be shown not just what he can see, but the likelyness that there is something he cannot see. This should also increase suspense when flying through areas that are supposably “deserted”.

**Each ship class/size/design should have a role to play on the battlefield.**

Most of this genre have a progressive ship quality problem. There is always a ship that is next to get and it always outclasses/mothballs all previous designs. Instead the player should move through the ship classes in a two-way motion based on his current position in the story/universe. Perhaps each ship class can be modified, customized or enhanced over time by spenind a lot of gameplay time piloting it. Different missions should require the player to think and choose what type of ship to use.

**Challenge pre-conceptions**

Most players would expect small ships to be fast and agile while the bigger ships pack a massive punch and have mile thick armor plates. This doesnt always have to hold true. Perhaps large ships are only used for industrial purposes and military craft are always corvette or fighter sized.

**Other ideas**

Large ships dont have high impulse FTL. Small-Mid sized ships can be “Pushed” to FTL for short periods of time. This is too expensive to utilize on large combat ships or cargo ships. These bigger ships must accelerate towards their target in a regular relitivistic way. Cargo and Capital Ships moving between points will be the focus of combat actions. Smaller craft can be launched from these ships or called in from stationary objects equipped with these “FTL Pushers”. This would create two distinct types of craft. Those small enough to be Pushed, and the rest would be as large as possible since once you cannot be pushed there is no reason to hold back.

Larger ship escorts would have to be dedicated to a flight before it leaves, and are tied up for the entire journey. Smaller ships could be used for backup and interdiction at the combat location, or to support ships moving away from the “pusher”. The smaller ships could be left with sufficient momentum to be able to rendevous with the friendly target, but could not be given a negative relative vector (which would allow them to come out of the pushed state with a vector toward the pusher”. Also only targets broadcasting a FTL beacon could be targetted, so that the exit points are accuracte.
