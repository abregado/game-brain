Brainstorming - Click Build Wait games

Time frame per game? months, weeks, days, hours  
Will players build up their game world over months of gameplay? Perhaps they can develop everything in just a few hours but are encouraged to try again a different way (replayability, iterative gameplay)

Replayability  
How to make the game interesting but replayable. Perhaps using a "open one door, close one other" technique.

Iterative Gameplay  
players play until they "lose" or until a time limit runs out. Then they must restart their persistant world.

Option to restart?  
Most of these games dont allow you to restart the game, only ever building up what you have and at the most restructuring everything (often more time consuming than a restart). If no option to restart exists then there must be an option to redecorate/organise for a reduced price/time investment.

fastest Rate of advancement  
Measured in time per game teir. Experience points and level requirements are a great way to regular how far a player is along in a game.

Rewards for what?  
just for clicking? for building? for leveling up? THose are the most common options. Perhaps rewards can also be given for rushing to a higher level/building a lot in a short space of time. Different play styles can be rewarded differently so as to encourage players playing how they like.

Just Click to get?  
Most of these games force the player to click on every game element to collect resources. Perhaps they must grab resources from the ground, or they can be collected with a single button press.

Minigames?  
Perhaps certain things require minigames or interactivity. To build something you might need to move resources around manually. Example being to build a new building you have to load up the game every few hours and manually move timber from a woodcutter to the building site.

Low art assest ideas  
Development costs should be considered. The programs should always be easy to finish in very short time frames

Multitouch use  
Intersting use of pre-existing technology. Intergrate all the useful features of a smart phone. Take advantage of smartphone and tablet portability. Camera, Bluetooth, multitouch, vibration sensors, pressure sensors, microphone, speakers, phone ability, GPS, battery life. Reward the player for using these technologies.

Persistance  
How persistant is the game world? How does the game communicate with the player? What needs to be stored over a long time. Can the players world change without his imput.

Local or Global  
Is the players world developed online or locally. Can the world be shared with others. Is there any reason to share with others.

interactivity between players  
Can players influence eachothers games? Can a player be "twinked" to a higher level.

Reasons for signing up your friends  
Other reasons related to gameplay. Most games give resource benifits based on how many friends you have playing the game. What can the game handle in terms of friend numbers\> How will you limit the number of friend benefits a player can get.

Community building  
Can multiple players build something together? Who is in charge? How are resources collected? Is the community project online or offline?

Cost to make a server, free to join a server  
Perhaps it is free to play in other peoples worlds but only paying customers can build worlds. Why would free players play? Incentives are needed for both playstyles.

Something to strive for  
Players must be able to see the goal and think "How do i get other there". There must be more than one goal so that the player feels like he has made a unique descision.

Endgame goals  
As the gameplay will not change throughout, what does the player have to play for at the endgame? Do they just stop playing?

Playing till bordem  
Do players just play your game until it is too slow to bother with? Can a player get into a regular rythm withthis game.

Fun to play or just interesting  
Is the game genuinely FUN? Perhaps it is just to ocupy someones mind for a few minutes in a workplace environment.

Player investments  
Does the player have to invest emotionally, or just invest a little of his time every so ften.

Uses for microtransactions  
The standard way is to pay to increase advancement speed. Other methods?

Concept 1: Invasion ClickBuildWaiter

Mechanics: consentual Player interaction, iterative gameplay, shared real world investment, community building, pay for privilage

Players tend plots of wild land, capturing and training animals they find. Wild land can be modified to attract creatures preferred by the player. Training your creatures sinply involves putting in resources and waiting. When you have a group of creatures trained and ready for combat you can send them off to fight, gaining an immediate reward. These groups go into a waiting list. Other players can call in attacks to train their own creatures or simply to have fun. When an attack is finished the player who defended gets a reward.

Players can also send creatures to town carrying resources for trade. These towns are run by paying players who also collect a tax on business carried out in their town. Playing players will want to make their town attractive to other players so that more business is carried out, and more players visit.

Town players also have a larger than normal wild land to play with. Inside the town they chose which buildings and services to place but these services only "level up" as other players use them. Wildlands players can also invest aditional cash in their favorite towns. Perhaps limiting the number of town players is a good idea.

Wild land players can restart with a new peice of wild land at anytime. When they do they gain bonus resources for their next land based on how many animal invasions they sent out, how much resources they spent on towns, and how well developed the land was. It should never be possible to have EVERYTHING on one peice of wildland.

Time frame for having a fully devgeloped wildland should be about 2 weeks of playing a little each night, or one week when you play it a lot.

Concept 2: Simple Iterative

Mechanics: Iterative gameplay, Local stored world, basic city builder mechanism

Players get a number of unique resources. These cannot be collected during the game. Each one allows the construction of a single structure. Structures are linked together and generate resources over time. Each structre has inputs and outputs. Each "city" only runs for 4 days and players cannot construct new buildings once it has begun working. If the city runs well enough the player gets more of the unique resources for their next city.

Later stages see players getting several rounds of building on the same city. More and more attention is required to get to the next level of unique resources.

Concept 4: Waiting exploration game  
Mechanics: Play and wait, push notifications, input Technology use, microtransactions

Players are exploring solar systems in a nebula. They select a destination and then a timer begins until they arrive. This timer counts down in real time but can be influenced by playing some minigames on the phone.

When the player "arrives" a push notification is sent to the phone. Any time after this the player can review the basic data about the solar system and decide to investigate the planets, asteroids, sun or any other anomalies present. Fill up their fuel and moving onto the next solar system. Data is stored on the phone for each location explored and can be backup up onto PC or Mac for offline review or uploading to a database.

Players can make microtransactions to speed up their ship or maybe to upgrade its statistics. Data is stored locally and so no online server is nessesary. Perhaps probes can be launched to multiple places to collect data before you move your ship, giving some more choices to the player.
