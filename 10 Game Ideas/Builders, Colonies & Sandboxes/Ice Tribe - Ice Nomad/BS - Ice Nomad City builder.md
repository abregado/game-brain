Other Stuff Ben was thinking of

**Abstract Representation of building huts**

Instead of having the tribesman construct huts and then work in them, we could have the output from each job increase over time to its maximum potential. This would promote planning ahead and having a “time investment” mechanic.

Which could be represented in many ways, and written off to fluff. In an Eskimo/ice world environment it could be that the longer a hut is populated the warmer it gets, allowing the tribesman to operate more efficiently.

Events would have to effect tribesman left in the camp otherwise we may run into “cookie cutter” problems with wikis and the best formula.

**Making the economy interesting**

Currently the system we have come up with gives the player choice of how many tribesman to apply to each of 4 jobs:

Gathering resources through the hunt

Converting meat into food (smoking/preparing/preserving)

Converting bone into tools (sharpening)

Converting skin into tents/bags (tanning/sewing)

This system is at risk of being optimized by the players very rapidly. Even with the instability generated through the events system, I dont believe it will hold up for very much gametime.

We can combat this by creating very interesting and balanced events, or by injecting something more into the actual economic model.

Personally I like systems where the players must strive to push for a goal, but are in danger if they overshoot such goals. Its much better for the player to feel that he cheated himself out of a victory than to think the system cheated against him.

Check a game called MULE

**Methods of removing “Mystery Meat”**

Recently I have been looking at titles and websites which suffer from Mystery Meat. This is a condition in which information is not made available to the player/user without them searching for a long time. Often important information is available only after hoving the mouse over a single pixel.

Steve thought we could have a “status bar” on one side of each screen showing all of the relevant numbers.

I thought that the less numbers we have on the screen the better for the players immersion.

In reality a combination of the two will have to be found.

The player needs to have some idea of how his choices will effect the game before pressing Continue.

**Event during travel**

I had thought there could be a Camp based event randomly during the Hunt Events. It may be better to have this occur during travel. Perhaps its outcome could effect where you set up camp. Different campsites might have variables effecting the number of hunters allowed, the efficiency of work carried out or even the speed at which the tribesman acclimatize. Other things could include extra food expense over time, promoting the player to move on very quickly.

This could also lead to events occurring during the “holmes step”

**Naming Convention for time in this game**

Since we are still unsure how long a time we want to simulate, perhaps we should call the units STUs. Subjective Time Units. In reality the player doesn’t need to know how long each time unit is since the tribesman not be regular humans, and the world not our regular world.

**New Stuff since the discussion with Vlad**

**Age being a big part**

Young and old are not effective at jobs

Do you leave your elders in the snow or have them eat your food and train your children?

Perhaps tribesman only live very few cycles (it is a harsh environment)

Possible idea for “training”

Upon returning to the holmes you are greeted by all the new children. The player is given several choices as to how to train the children, with some choices being much harder than others. The harder choices yield better training rewards but injure and/or kill the weaker children. This would be a method of filtering based on your desire for quantity or quality. Of course we wouldn't explicitly have child massacres going on, but imply some sort of harsh training regime where the children simply “fail”.

**Asymmetry between phases of the cycle**

Do we keep each phase of the cycle similar to allow more increase in player skill over time, or perhaps we have special “seasons” where different options are available.

Three seasons out hunting and moving, followed by a winter season where you return to the women and children to feed them and stay in a cave (holmes). Here different choices might have to be made to prepare for the following three seasons

Note: No need for exactly 4 seasons.

Perhaps each event while hunting could be linked to particular seasons. Some creatures might not be found in every area during every seasons, prompting the player to make different choices as they wont be optimal at a later/earlier stage of the cycle.

**Weather/Cycle time penalties**

If you wait too long, the sun catches up to you, making everything dead

If you move too fast you are hunting in a cold desolate tundra

The player wants to move at the optimal speed, but the events he is presented with may force him to move on early or late.

**Creature Population**

Perhaps the rate at which creatures can reproduce is tracked, so that killing too many of them in the wrong season can actually cause them to become scarce.. more metagaming during events may occur.

**KoDP style events for hunt**

Perhaps multiple minievents chosen once the hunters return

Minievents effect:

Time until the hunt returns

injuries to the hunters

ratio of Meat/Bone/Skin for spoils

Maximum carrying capacity

Loss/damage of tools and supplies

these come in Combinations of three. The player selects the outcome of each consecutively without knowing what the next event will be. Choosing the “best” option in an early event might force you to take a “bad” option on the last event.

There should be one objectively bad decision in each event, but never an objectively good one.

**Basic ideas for economic gameplay**

Tribesman in the camp convert skins and bones into tools, equipment and housing at a fixed rate

Having not enough tribesman in a hut means it gets cold at night

Having too many tribesman in a hut makes it crowded and inefficent to work in

Huts are also used to store resources and commodities

Parties(?) can be thrown to give benefits but consume more food. Could also be tribal rituals and blessings.

**Big descisions include:**

How many tribesman to send on the hunt

How to allocate the remaining between:

Building new huts

Processing hunt spoils

Preparing food

Defending the camp

How long to stay at the camp (penalties for too short/too long?)

Which items to take to the next camp

**Phases in the camp screen**

Time passes in large discrete units. Each turn is either a day or a night and these are the smallest measurement of time. Could be interesting to add different things to do in day and night:

create a work roster for the daytime

create a sleep roster for night

decide is the camp eats once in day , once at night, only one, or not at all

**Pressures**

Encroaching climate - harsher conditions force the hunt further afield, less time to hunt per round.

Diminishing animal population - more dangerous animals.

Environmental cycling - difficulty increasing, then luls before increasing again. Always harder, but with breaks. Ways to inform player of impending ease - plant life, shamans, elders, events.

Size of tribe, mf population ratios, more women and children harder to feed.

Increasing size of stockpile required to last the winter.

**Gameplay Time Estimate**

~30 decision per cycle, plus summaries, ~10 minutes per cycle.

Most resources are from hunted animals and fish. Strategy comes from how to use each resource as it can only be used once. More manpower means more resources, but also means more resources consumed to maintain the status quo. The player must divide his people into one group to go out hunting, and the other to converting resources at home and constructing new buildings (cutting ice bricks). Animals may also attack the camp so it is risky to send too many people and weapons out hunting.

Every three camps, the tribe returns to its ancestral home for the Big Cold. They cannot hunt, only making crafts/trades. Enough food must be stored to last this time. The Big Cold can sometimes go for longer or shorter times, so bringing extra food ensures your tribe wont be effected. However, if you dont bring enough other resources back, your tribesman wont be as productive during this phase, and may become bored. Children are born during this time based on sleeping arrangements throughout the other three camps. Couples are created as well.

Each camp is further away from the ancestral home and has different amounts of each animal. Hunting becomes more successful the further you travel. The first camp is only about collecting enough food to make the next journey. The Second camp is where you get much of your basic resources, and the third is rich enough that you can focus how you like and plan for the Big Cold.

Other Ideas

Each new area for a temporary village has a number of good places to build igloos and some further, less valuable locations. These could suffer a penalty to build speed, or actually cause industries within to go slower.

Some areas could also have lower quality ice so that the final structure is not as warm as the normal.

Players must choose which spots to use for what industries. The most important industries of course going in the best spots.

Igloo size could also be different between building spots.

Maybe a Totem is carried by the leader tribal and planted in the centre of the map. Efficency is dictated by distance from the totem. Also means that the first tribal cannot carry anything else to the next area (small diminishing returns mechanics)

Basic Survival City Builder

Codename: Icetribe

Philosophy

Simple cyclical gameplay.

Three resources

Every resource can be used multiple ways

Every resource is consumed to maintain status as well as used to advance

Player should spend the first cycle learning all of the mechanics

Later cycles to better his game

nothing ground breaking, just simple mechanics

easy to use interface!

Meat. Consumed as Food. Used to allow travel after each cycle

Bone. Consumed to make tools as they break. Used to make both useful and cosmetic items (score)

Fur. Consumed to make clothes for increasing population. Used to make shelter, and to increase population capacity. Also used to increase carrying capacity.

Other "resources"

Manpower. More tribals means more resource gathering and transformations. Increases the rate at which you must use resources for maintenace

Ice. Use manpower to cut icebricks. These are used to build your nomadic village up.

Enclosed Space. The more space inside your icehuts means more space to use for storage, sleeping, working. Of course each hut can only be used for one job, nobody likes to sleep on the floor of the butchery hut!

Player choices

Plan ice huts. When your tribe arrives at a new location you design the first of your nomad ice village. The bigger the hut the more room inside, but also the longer it takes to build. The benifits from a larger hut are great but the time it takes becomes exponentially longer and there is always the chance of collapse if you try to build them too large.

Which tribals go hunting, which stay to perform resource transformations. Those that go hunting take a variable time (unknown to player) to return. When they do, you can see a report of what they encountered and what they have brought back. Those that stay behind are tasked with preparing the raw materials from previous hunts. Jobs include Smoking meat, carving bone tools and weapons, tanning hides to make blankets, clothes and tents

How much to carry with you. Everything you take to the next location must be carried by the tribals. Each cycle you must decide carefully what to leave behind as you can never take everything!
