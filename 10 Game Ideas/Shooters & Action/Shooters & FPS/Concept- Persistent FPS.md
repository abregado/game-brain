# The Point of Persistence

The point of persistence is to increase the value of game rewards and penalties. Players know that the rewards they get increase their game “wealth” and this wealth will be available for them to use forever. Many online games give players many many irrelevant rewards, but only some that are of any real gameplay value. This seems anti-thesis to the persistence model.

Example: receiving hundred of loot items, only to pick out the useful ones in WoW

Persistence can also be used to give meaning to time spent in the game world. Often this is the case with subscription based games where the aim is to keep the player coming back, month in, month out.

# Relevant FPS Rewards

This is a tricky ideal. In FPS the only relevant reward is an advantage over your opponent. Some ideas include:

- Higher Tiered items

- weapon upgrades

- more powerful ammunition

- Powerups (temporary, random or match duration)

- Additional HUD information

- Information on player equipment (scanner)

- Map Choice or Veto

- Match Condition Choice

- Opponent Choice

- damage resistance equipment

- resistance to particular items

- disabling others equipment

- effecting stats of equipment

- random events

- Map modifications

- dirty tricks

- trap placement in level

- bullet time :D

- recharging selected abilities

- Triggerabile abilities/skills/powers

- increase respawns (multiplicity)

# Luck or Skill in FPS?

Both have their place. Over all FPS should be about reflexes, tactics and area knowledge. These are all player skills. Luck is best kept for character skills, which should never be the major factor in a battle. Timing and clever use of said character skills should always be more important that continuously cycling. Maybe the best way to keep this true is to have limited quantities of everthing.

# Controlling Inflation in a FPS Persistent world

A major issue with persistant worlds is inflation. Any time items are introduced into the system faster than they are removed, the value of said items will decrease. The only ways to combat this are:

- Remove items from circulation

- Increase demand for the items (more consumers)

- Allow items to be recycled into others with more demand

- Increase the interest rate (in real life)

- Make the consumers poorer (tax)

- Other money sinks to remove wealth

- Limit the supply of money and items

- Increase consumption (more consumables)

My preferred ways would be to make all items/skills/weapons/armor/effects be usable a limited number of times (ammunition/charges). To promote players keeping only what they want and not building up a stock of useless items, they should be charged to store excess equipment.

2018: paying for storage seems like drudge work and we should be able to design a better system for this.

# Player Rewards

In a PVP game with any sort of market, rewards cannot be given only to winners. This would unbalance the population very rapidly. Opportunities for reward must be given to all players regardless of a matches outcome. In the instance of a Persistent Deathmatch FPS rewards should be roughly equal for all parties, with the winner receiving perhaps 10-20% more than the losers. Extra reward could be given to the player who disadvantages himself the most. Other reward situations include:

- Getting kills in difficult places

- Paying for rewards before a game (investing wealth)

- Betting on the outcome

- Fufilling situational criteria

- Not repeating kill styles

- using less gear than your opponent

- using less ammunition than your opponent

- inflicting the most damage.

Rewards could also be limited to the number of new players. As new players join, the total wealth in the system increases. As players spend into money sinks, the system can be topped up with new rewards. This dynamic system should see player rewards fluctuating. If players hoard rewards, they will also be spending more on money sinks. These elements should keep items moving through the economy.

# Player trading

Trading allow players to collect the items they prefer. Keeping trading popular will also see the cost of items balanced by the players preferences. This also rewards players for using weapons that are not popular. Trading should not just be a dump where players can leave unwanted items. Some ideas for trading include:

- Auctions

- Direct Trading

- Guild/Player Group Sharing

- Sale to NPC shop

- Sale to player run shops

- Open Market

Making the trading interface complicated, or having too many options will not suit a fast paced FPS shooter. Players should spend as little time as possible offloading their unwanted equipment.

2018: only allow trading between parties who just fought, so value of items remains fluid?

# Item Crafting

Crafting items from raw materials does not lend itself very well to an FPS. Other ideas for transmuting items into others include:

- Breaking items down into parts

- Parts can be combined into other items

- Crafting must take place during a match

- Each player earns transmutation “points”

- equipment can only be broken down into consumable items

- Items have ingredients, new items can be made by combining items without breaking them down

- Only addons can be crafted, base items must be “proc'd” or traded

I like the idea of players being able to craft consumables from unwanted items instead of just selling them. This process would have to be inefficent to avoid being a better option than attempting to trade. Crafting during a match would have to relate to completing objectives and could be uncontrollable.

The best combination could be breaking items down into consumables and “transmutation Points”. A player is given several of these and can use them to transform several items into one of his choice. This could also be a good way to let long time players get the items they preferr to use, even if these are quit expensive at the time. It may also prove to be a controllable economy leveller.

# Experience and Player Progression

As discussed this Persistent FPS should be based on player skill. Player progression comes from learning to use each weapon to maximum effect, as well as learning how to counter different combinations.

Players earn new equipment and become familiar with said equipment. As the efficency of this combination increases, so will a players ability to win matches.

Players will also learn the rewards system and use it to earn the maximum amount of rewards as they play. This will allow them to experience all the combinations much faster, as well as select the combinations they prefer easier. In a way, winners will control the economy by getting first pick of new randomly generated items.

# Penalty for Death

Several options exist for this category. A permadeath FPS shooter could be quite thrilling as no character will survive forever. Players could also lose all of the equipment that they took into battle when they lose, in hardcore gaming fashion. Failing to be competative should not cause a player to become helpless, regardless of how many times it happens.

Losing might see you get no choice over the rewards you get. Perhaps the winning player can see and choose from a large pool of rewards, the dregs of which go to the next player, and so on until the last player gets no choice. This would mean that players are always progressing, while winning players are progressing in the direction they wish. Seeing as specialization and honeing your skill is the only way to get better at the game, it might just work.

The game could also be divided into ranked and unranked matches. In unranked matches players can battle without fear of losing anything, while in ranked matches the player stands to gain glory, fame and rewards if they win, and certain death if they lose.

Player might also be asked to wager on permadeath. Selecting to use less “respawns” could give other advantages. When final death does occur the penalties might be more severe for those who chose the greater number of respawns.

# Self Balancing Gameplay

By utilizating a system of wagering, players could be made to balance themselves against their opponents. If a player is not penalised for taking excessive amounts of equipment and ammunition they will always seek to bring everything they can. Players would bring their best weapons and equipment to every fight. However if the losing player loses everything after the fight, and the winner also looses unused respawns and ammunition, then all players will seek to win with the least equipment/ammunition/respawns possible.

This could be balanced against the costs of keeping more equipment than you use in inventory. Players could be responsible for economy AND gameplay balance. Any self regulating system is better than one where the game developers need to constantly interfere.
