# Coop Ship Repair

A team of couch friends risk their doom while repairing a broken spaceship

Ben Buckton

### Introduction

Explore derelict ships and space platforms in this couch co-op, top down puzzle game. Each derelict needs first to be explored, and then triaged to find what is wrong with it. Finally the crew will need to restart all of the systems, replace broken components, and reroute power between damaged areas.

But beware, every derelict has a countdown until something catastrophic happens.

### Gameplay Story

This old freighter is beaten up pretty bad. First I found that the drive reactor was shut down due to the radiation shielding not being powered. So I ran a new cable from the shielding to the backup generators. The problem is, as soon as I turn on the drive reactor, the fire suppression system will reboot, cycling all of the blast doors. This will cut the cable I ran, disabling the shielding and flooding the ship with radiation.

Disabling the fire suppression would work, but what happens if the drive reactor is damaged and floods the ship with plasma?

### Background

Demand is growing for coop games faster than they are being produced. Those that do exist tend to fall into the survival crafting genre, and have very long play times. I would like to play a game that is designed for shorter sessions.

Games such as Out of Space and Tools Up show that such games can work. I would like to see if such a game will be successful while having a more serious space theme.

### Key Features

- **Round based gameplay:** ~15 minute rounds

- **Equipment based Exploration**: Explore the wreckage and add cameras to each room to increase your vision.

- **Information gameplay:** Learn, Diagnose, Repair. First you need to find out how the ship should work, then figure out what has gone wrong. Once you know this, the repairs can begin.

- **Equipment based Roles:** Each player selects a suit with a set of tools for specialized tasks.

### Genre and Theme

Genre: Information based puzzler

The game would have a top down perspective to better allow local co-op. The mood would be a lot more dark than similar games in this genre. Players have limited visibility to begin with, uncovering the map as they explore. This should give the game a more tense feeling.

The art should be clean, hard tech,. Examples of this style would be games like Stationeers or series such as The Expanse (martian ships).

### Technology

Engine choice could be anything that supports controllers. For this reason I would go for Unity with Top Down Engine. This also opens up more options for platform support.

The game would be 2.5D to increase availability of placeholder assets.

### Platform

Although more serious space games tend to target PC audiences, the game would be designed to work with Controllers primarily and support Keyboard/Mouse controls. Both of these map well to touch screens, therefore we could theoretically deploy to any console or platform.

### Market

We will be targeting the midpoint between casual couch co-op gamers and players of strategic space builder games. We would hope to attract the strategic player by offering a more relaxed and short experience they can share with friends. For the casual players, we would offer a less cartoonish environment and a little more tension.

### Development

The proof of concept needs to be made over the course of a single week long game jam. I suspect that this will be long enough if there are two developers and simple assets are used. An optional third team member, an artist could also be helpful.

If no artist is available, then the project could also be completed without art. The concept that needs to be proven is the gameplay itself, rather than the theming and style. In this case, sound will be more important than graphics.

### Do

- Keep each feature short. A title and one sentence is enough. If there is some cool interaction between two features, it should be in the Player Story.

- Setting and Story (if a selling point) should be mentioned throughout the document.

- Include number of players somewhere, but not as a feature unless it is a selling point.

- Hint at what the player will feel when interacting with feature X.

- Intro and Tagline should hint at Design pillars.

- Be honest if you have not considered one of the points, rather than just removing the section.

- Be honest with your development constraints, an interested publisher will want to help.

### Don't

- Pretend that your game is completely unique

- Explain how features will work (instead explain why they are important)

- Write the Intro and Tagline first, they are the most important things!

- Include interface/control information (unless it is different, then it is a design feature)

- Include music/sound information (unless it is a selling point)

- Include anything you would describe just for the sake of pointing out the thing will be good. EG:// “The game will have good sound/controls/graphics”.
