## Unique selling point

Before a run starts, a player is shown a screen with several implants.

The player can choose which (non, some, all) cybernetic implants to take. The starting character can have any or all of these implants, allowing the player to decide how hard the game will be.

However taking more implants means that the players batteries will run out faster, placing a time limit mechanic into the game. The easier the player makes it, the less potential he has to have a long game.

As players master different parts of the game, they will want to take less and less implants to enable themselves to survive for longer. But which ones they chose to remove also changes the gameplay every time.

Eventually the player can attempt to survive without any implants.

## Regular roguelike features:

- early game is tense

- hunting creatures for food

- procedural terrain

- stamina tracking

- limited carrying capacity

## Differences from other Roguelikes

- Slow backwards and strafing movement

- fast, long action system using food for fuel (fast travel)

- detailed information gameplay: sound, smell, sight, heat

- three stage running. Walk, Sprint and autorun cruise (maximize speed without draining stamina)

- Players don’t start out with nothing

- No skills

- crashed ship acts as starting shelter and a place to sleep

- non-HP health system

- Tool charge instead of tool durability

- Flat difficulty curve over play time while the player learns and takes their “training wheels” off.

## Biomes

All pretty boring actually.

- abandoned colony

- glacier w/caves

- underground lavaflow

- frozen mountain pass

- tundra expanse

Players starting point is his ship which has crash landed on the planet. Each time a new change is started, the ship crashes in a different place. This is to extend the learning time without creating monotony in the first few games.

Players listing on the scoreboard should detail which implants were used.
