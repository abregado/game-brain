Terrain comprises an assortment of randomly placed and shaped geometry

the player has a number of turret objects which can be repacked and moved very slowly

these also take a while to redeploy

they can only fire when deployed

(See similar Ideas I had for reactionary enemy spawns based on extraction amounts)

structures are placed on the map. These give a bonus to ajoined structures and turrets

only defensive structures can be moved

structure strategy comes from managing throughput and managing waste outputs

resources gathered need to be moved to a processor to be made smaller and then brought to a starport or teleporters to be converted into score

The scoring and pacing should be such that:

- a player who extracts faster builds up threat faster

- taking on more risk is the way to higher end scores

- pre-planning a strategy and choosing an extraction rate to suit should be a major advantage

Levels are generated randomly and the player can select from a number of missions/contract to attempt. Each has a time limit, a quantity of resources required and a list of disadvantages the player will have to deal with. These disadvantages could be limited number of structures/turrets, or efficency reductions.

3-5 of these missions could be listed, in ascending order of difficulty/reward.

As players progress they will build up stocks of structures, turrets and active abilities. These are purchased between rounds. These have a fixed cost and a maintenece cost if deployed into a contract zone. During each contract a player can order/build other structures however it will cost more than if he/she purchased it if it is destroyed and the maintenance costs are also higher (rental fee?). Insured version could also be purchased for much higher rental fee, but cost less if lost.

Buying upgrades means making more XP/cash from a successfully completed contract, and selecting contracts that suit your inventory of equipment.

if a contract is not completed you can still sell the collected materials for a lower per unit value. As this might not cover your running costs, it is important to know when you are falling behind and bail out early.

# Multiplayer Ideas

This might work well in multiplayer if you have several people working cooperativly to complete larger contracts.

Also other players could sell/rent their equipment out, taking all the risks that go with it.
