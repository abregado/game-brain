# Ultratech lawn village

## Setting description

The sky is illuminated in the day, and is dark at night, but no sun or moon can be seen. It rains lightly on every 11th night.

A large village. Around 1000 residents. Near to the village is a small forest, and a lake which is fed by an underground spring. Surrounding the village in all directions are farmlands, for about 1 km. Beyond this is almost perfectly flat grasslands, covered in short lawn grass. No roads can be seen. Nothing on the horizon.

The village is not mediaeval. In fact, life here is quite advanced. There is no money, as food and consumable objects are in abundance. Important equipment is shared as needed. Life is easy, and people live a long and happy life.

The reason for the easy lifestyle and abundance are the Tools. Irreplaceable and uncraftable objects of power which allow a person to do the job of many people and mostly without training. This means that most villagers only work one day a week, and often a different task every week. On the other days, the villagers spend time writing or creating art.

What can not be seen in the village are vehicles or animals of any kind. Tools and resources are heavy but are always moved by hand. Some of the tools cannot be moved at all, seemingly attached to the ground where they are. Since people have to work so little, they are happy to just move things manually.

The village has few rules but the most important ones are:

Do not break the Tools.

Do not take the Tools without permission

The Tools do not leave the village

## Themes for this setting

Living in an artificial world, Post scarcity, meaning of one’s life without work, non-violent challenges, generational ignorance and superstition in a technological world, social pressure without violence, hunger or oppression. Human labour multiplication without automation.

## Inciting incidents for this setting

### The Stranger

A stranger arrives at the village one morning. It is not a surprise as some villagers saw them appear on the horizon. The oldest in the village say they cannot remember someone arriving in their lifetime.

The stranger has a few small Tools.

Options for why the stranger is here.

- They, suspiciously, have some resource not found here that was running low.

- They want to trade a tool for supplies to continue their journey

- They came to take one or more villagers with them.

Most questions asked to the stranger end in the same answer: “I cannot say. Secrets are for keeping. If I tell, the way onwards will be closed to me”

### Pioneer

One of the villagers decides that they want to leave because:

- They don’t enjoy their life in the village

- Never before heard of Wanderlust

- For explorations sake

- They want to find other people

### Banished

A character breaks a tool, accidentally or on purpose. Due to this:

- a group of villagers must leave to lower the population.

- The “criminal” must leave

- A group of villagers try to monopolise the remaining tools of that type.

### Plenty and Expansion

For some reason, a new tool is discovered or is created. This means that the amount of surplus generated has increased. This might mean:

- That a traveller could take one of the tools with them

- The population can grow more suddenly

- Something comes to sort out the imbalance

- A different tool must be chosen to be disabled, creating a political choice

# Preseeded Planet

## Setting Description

Humanity sent out Von Neumann probes to several solar systems to begin preparing the planets there for human settlement. Upon arriving at one such planet, the human settlers discovered that the probe sent here had constructed large structures in a regular, geometric formation across the entire surface of the planet.

When landing on the surface to begin colonisation, it becomes clear that the probe has retained its orders to prepare the planet for human arrival, but does not see the settlers as human any more.

The structures on the surface seem entirely dormant until humans approach them, after which they come alive and begin removing anything that they see as non-natural, non-human from the area.

Upon closer inspection, each of these structures is controlled by a separate copy of the original program. Each has a set of rules which have mutated somewhat from the original.

The settlers find a place to start building where the programs surrounding them tolerate their presence… somewhat.

## Themes for this setting

Rock and a hard place. What counts as human, artificial protecting the natural. The damage is already done. Impossible task. The indigenous are robots. The big oops. Pseudo benevolent AI overloads. Many Gaias

## Inciting incidents

### Colonial Tax

The colonial fleet will be returning in a few years and will expect progress to have been made, pushing the colonists to:

- Begin destroying the structures

- Exploit the areas they already have access to, drawing the ire of programs further away

- Want to leave

### The big secret

The colonists discover that humans have already been on this planet because:

- The robots were not sent by humans

- Humans are sent here regularly to test them for something

- This is actually Earth

- The programs are uploaded humans

- The machines are actually running the show, and want toys

### Pack your bags

The characters need to go on a grand journey where the main issue will be dealing with robots created by the programs along the path. Their destination could be:

- A program structure that recognises them as humans

- A master reset switch (Big payoff)

- The colonial fleet crashed but there is a working shuttle to get them off the planet

### Triggered

Something one of the characters does triggers the programs to act differently towards humans. They could:

- Start treating humans as animals to be managed (culling, breeding, protecting)

- Start ignoring humans

- Begin a massive colonisation effort, ignoring the existing humans in the process

- Completely dismantle themselves, thinking their job is complete. Creates a power vacuum.

- Begin fighting with other programs.

### Chosen One

One of the characters is recognised by the programs as human which means they have far more power than other humans on this planet. This could be a villain or a protagonist in the story.

# Divisive Monoliths

## Setting

Earth. Sometime in history or the future.

The monoliths appeared all at once. Either raining down from space or raising up from the earth. Some even say they just appeared where they now stand, phasing in from some otherworldly place. Each perfectly pentagonal in cross section, 5 kilometres to a side. Each is at least 10 kilometres tall. And black, so perfectly black.

For days or weeks, they stood inert. Humanity reeling from the sudden knowledge their works are insignificant. Humanity mourning from the sudden deaths of many millions.

Then the waves started. Short, invisible pulses of energy emitted at seemingly random times between the Monoliths. Organic matter is annihilated, and even the most hardened electronics are fried. The land between the Monoliths in 5 kilometre wide strips is wiped clean.

Even outside these areas, the waves cause so much interference that aeroplane travel becomes impossible and wireless communication is unreliable. But these are only the first symptoms of the isolation that humanity is going to face. The Zones are born.

Zones with cities but no farmland are soon crippled due to starvation. Zones containing two cultures with generational hatreds are consumed with internal wars. Some Zones manage to adapt and flourish.

Many people flee their zone. Crossing a Boundary is doable. Five kilometres is far on foot, but with a bicycle you can manage it before the next wave comes. If you have a working motor vehicle then it is even easier, if those in your current zone will allow you to take such resources with you. Those who fail to cross never see their deaths coming. The waves are invisible and annihilation is certain.

Wars end. Countries end. Some might say it is for the best.

## Themes for this setting

Isolation. National self-reliance. A world without trade. Enigmatic horror. Overwhelming non-human but docile oppressors. Risking everything to flee your situation. Material representation of cultural divides. Abstracted Geopolitics.

## Inciting events

### Refugees (leaving)

The characters are fleeing from their current zone to another zone. To cross the barrier they will need supplies, vehicles and an acceptance of their own death. The reason they are leaving is:

- Their zone is oppressive

- They know of an imminent event that will cause chaos in the zone

- Someone is about to nuke their zone

- They suspect they have a way to calculate a wave-free window to cross.

- They are looking for someone in another zone

### Refugees (arriving)

The characters have just crossed a zone into unknown territory. They don’t know the customs or laws here, have no friends and need supplies. They find a zone that:

- Is highly suspicious of people from other zones

- Worships the monoliths, and glorifies those who cross safely

- Is suspiciously thriving, with a working power grid, communications and utilities

- Highly militarised

- Has reverted to an agrarian culture

- Contains a city which is built to avoid the shadow of the nearest monolith

- Covered by alien plants

### Statecraft

The leaders of a large city in a zone cut off from some of what it needs must maintain control over its population and adapt to the new situation.

### Courier

A character has been sent to deliver a very valuable piece of information or an item to someone several zones away.

### Secret Immunity

One or more characters learn that they are immune to the barrier waves. This leads to a situation where:

- The characters are conscripted as couriers.

- The characters need to keep everyone from finding out.

- Regular citizens are put into the barrier to find out if they are also immune.

- The characters become wealthy from salvaging items inside the barriers.

# Underground Utopia

Somewhere deep underground lives a culture. Life is good and easy in the culture. The citizens work, but they can choose to do whatever they feel is valuable for the society. Eventually, most citizens discover their Calling, which drives them to master a particular type of work.

After discovering their Calling, and mastering everything known about a trade, there is rarely enough time left in a citizen's life to create a breakthrough. But breakthroughs are not really the goal, since life is already easy and people are happy. Progress is slow, and steady.

There are a few, however, who never find their Calling. Such people are encouraged to try many different professions or to make up new ones. Some of these are content to just live their lives, enjoy their relationships and eventually die Uncalled.

Even fewer are the Outcasts. Citizens take on the name Outcast when they agree to leave the underground and, using one of the many ancient shafts, head to the surface. These are citizens who are so unsettled by the idea of dying Uncalled, that they are willing to risk dying early and alone.

The society still supports Outcasts, giving them plenty of supplies and standing prepared to welcome them back home whenever they decide to return.

The vast majority of Outcasts do return, sworn not to talk about their adventures.

But some stay on the surface forever.

## Themes

Self imposed isolation. Finding one's place in society. Post scarcity culture. Secrets of the outside world. Plato’s cave. Adventure.

## Inciting incidents

### Riding the elevator

One or more characters decide to be Outcast and pack their things onto an elevator for the surface.

### Mouthy returners

One or more Outcasts have returned to the society and somehow word has gotten around of what they encountered. This triggers a group of characters who are already found their Calling to go to the surface.

### Search for love

The partner of a character who found their Calling decides to become an Outcast. After several months, the character realises that their partner was their true Calling and leaves to find them.
