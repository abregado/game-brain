Dan Harmons Circle

Who, Need, Go (Chaos Entry), Search, Find, Take, Return (Order Entry), Change

Describe, don’t explain

Know how to sum up what your character wants in one sentence.

Character wants to \[goal\], but \[obstacle\], therefore \[conflict\].

Open with a status update (Who, where is here)

Heighten the tension and the stakes

End with a promise about the next scene

White Room/Talking Heads Syndrome

Add background input to dialogue heavy scenes.

Every line needs to serve at least one purpose.

What is a character actually trying to say? (What is the subtext?)

Themes always should be delivered through subtext (show instead of tell).

Conversation not Dialogue.

Anchor each NPC with ONE quality or reaction or emotion.

Combine this with a goal the NPC has.

Everything else can be contradictory.

NPCs do what is good for them, not what is good for the plot.

What happens if the heroes walk away?

Plotting Pitfalls

- Lack of Personal Stakes (What do the characters stand to LOSE)

  - Specific to the heros

  - Give the heroes something to care about

- Unfocus trajectory

  - External and internal goals overlap

  - How do I plan the heroes goals to change over time?

- Slow middle

  - Signs of:

    - Heroes are comfortable

    - Unsure of what to do

    - Just waiting for something

    - Travelling over a vast landscape

    - Worrying about the same things over and over

    - Experiencing repetitive conflict

    - Circular conversations

  - Do we need a midpoint reversal?

- Unsatisfying Payoffs

  - Deliver on promises of drama

  - What questions are the players excited about?

  - Big finish

    - Plot twists

    - Surprise Death (only if it serves the plot or theme)

    - Earned triumph

    - 

Rules of Showing

1.  Use evidence to support your claims

2.  Replace abstract with Concrete

    1.  Actions which indicate emotion instead of started emotions

    2.  Be wary of description including opinion based words (judgements)

3.  Substitute vague descriptions with sensory details

4.  Avoid relying too much of body language

5.  Show emotion through dialogue

6.  Filter observations through the narrative voice

Setting

Some time after the previous campaign. The barriers between worlds have started to unravel. Cities have become cut off from one another. Some areas have dissolved completely into the elemental chaos.

The Heroes arrive in Neverwinter by Spelljammer.

Big world points and proofs:

- This is the same world as before

  - A statue in Thundertree of Livia

  - The Echo is a tavern in Phandalin

  - The Spellforge is still operating, and is one reason that the area is still solid

- The world is dissolving into the Elemental Chaos

  - The players should need to travel to the edge early on

Behaviors I want to encourage with rules

Players setting up dramatic moments for other players

Players coming up with interesting ways to avoid combat

Campaign wide rules

To build foreboding

**Compounding Luck**

When a player rolls a critical failure, they are asked “Does Tymora smile on you?” If they answer yes, then the critical failure does nothing, but the GM makes a mark against the whole party. Tally is kept openly so the party can see how many they have accrued.

Time as currency

Each time the party travels, they add a number of days to a tally that is kept under each quest.

Boss: The heros will fight ONE of these

Hunter: An enemy that appears if the heroes are noticed.

Guard: Commonly one is encountered with several minions. These are less stupid than minions.

Minion: Basic foot soldier. Stupid and weak

Pet: Possibly stronger than the Minions, but adds flavor to the group dynamics. Found alone, chained up or under the control of a Guard/Hunter.
