## Basics

Design Rules

- No knowledge of Retrogames should be needed

- playtime 1.5 hours for most groups

- always have at least 2 puzzle chains available to be worked on

- Playing the computer games should not give hints but be necessary (hints may be missed during gameplay).

- Easy to produce!!!! We have no time for fancy shit

- Use Tron Theme

Materials:

- Lots of colorful printing (easy access and Yodas specialty)

- Blocks and Cubes!

- Much larger boxes for the main ‘chests’, of which there are 4 and can be visible immediately. Perhaps large metal colorful boxes

- NO LANGUAGE! These puzzles should have no english or german text. Ok, maybe GAME OVER or LOADING or some other stuff.

Use Tron Theme

Thematic Ideas

- Everything is movable except the 4 boxes and the Computer

- This perhaps gives the fixed things a sense of importance as in a videogame

- Grid paint on walls

- No furniture at all. Only Stacks of things, some large enough to sit on

- Completely plain (but grid painted) walls. Nothing Hanging

- The Computer could be a “Arcade Machine from Hell” with the top half rotated 180 degrees.

- If it is in the middle of the room it will be creepy

- No Fog needed! YEY!

- Mazes and Game Boards are on the bottom of large Foam Cubes so the players need to search underneath.

- Cubes that are not flat on the ground but ‘floating’ 1cm off the ground with underlighting.

## Puzzle Ideas

### One Large PACMAN-esque Maze, printed.

Several Colored Paths through Maze

Each path starts at the same place, goes over powerups and ends at an enemy

Find the effect and points value of each powerup to determine how many points that player got. Computer asks for the color of one player

**Parts:** Scraps of paper that show the points value of different powerups and which powerups eat which monsters. Magnetic parts which can stick onto the maze to show path locations or new powerups.

How about: No colored paths in the maze. Different colored players and destinations and pills on the way. The question is: How many pills does the red/blue/yellow player have to eat to get to point A/B/C? So the players have to find the shortest way and count the pills on the way. Result is a number. Or more than one number.

### Dungeon Maze - Printed or Lazercutwood

Find pieces of paper that show levels of a dungeon.

Based on which hero you put at the top, the depth that the hero gets to is different

Power Ups effect where the player dies

Computer asks for one hero and depth when they died

Parts: The Dungeon levels themselves, the legend of powerups and enemies

### Minecraft Recipes

Find Minecraft Recipes to show products and score.

Recipes found on blocks in the room

Use these recipes for basic puzzles in the room

Computer asks number of blocks to make something, or asks if it is possible to build X out of a pile of blocks

### Jigsaw Maze

Maze with square sections missing. Each gap has unique numbers on them. Each found piece has 2 sides with different parts of the maze. Find a combination of numbers on a clue sheet and then determine the ending point. Similar to the Black Statue puzzle in the Pharaoh's tomb but on a larger scale with more theming.

### Roborally Maze

A Map with numerous starting and ending positions, and a number of ‘pushers’ aka logic moves. Players find a series of cards with instructions on each. Card series are color coded so players can sort cards into series. The cards are also numbered so you can tell the order. Once you find a full series you can calculate on the board the outcome. Each series could generate multiple solutions based on different starting locations.

### One Liners

Other simpler Questions and Puzzles for the Computer to ask

- Spatial Relationships between objects in the room

- Small Blocks being stacked in different ways

- Holders for collections of parts, rather than one table area. This causes the players to move back and forth in the room, which should increase the excitement.

- NO CIPHERS OR READING!

- Pokemon evolution pictures (there are some obvious evolution chains)

- Minecraft sword and pickaxe in room with a chunk missing to hold a ‘pixelated gem’. Find 3 Gems with numbers. Each Gem in each tool gives a different result. Computer asks for Gem-Tool Combination.

- Loads of items available in room but placed randomly. Give obvious locations where things can be placed just to give the OCD players something to do.

### Hiding in plain sight

- A push activated Schubladen like Alex made for the coffee stuff

- A false wall with some secret doors like the Spint we built.

- Removable panels that are held on with Kettverschluss.

- Panels that can be tilted a little to reveal information in one corner.

- Panels that can be slid up to reveal clues (gravity lock)

## The 3 Path Flow Diagram



### Explanation

The Idea here is that there are 3 paths through the game that are separate MOST of the time. At two points the paths come together and ‘trade clues’. With 6 players, all paths can be played at the same time. Two of the paths can be completed without playing through the Games Path.

The nodes on the diagram are only for explanation purposes. The actual puzzle pieces would be different.

This creates a different flow based on player numbers:

- A 2 player team would move back and forth between the two paths and then when they finished the two puzzle paths they will play through the Games path

- A 3-4 Player team could play both main paths at once as 2 smaller teams, and then once finished they could play though the Game Path. Otherwise if one team is faster with their Path, this team would start on the Games Path to unlock the next quiz.

- a 5-6 player team can do all three Paths at the same time.

### Points to tell players about this system

- ALL PLAYERS should be present when a large box is opened, as there are components for all paths.

- There is always more than one puzzle to be worked on at the same time

- The Games can come at the end if they like

### Other things

- For 1-2 players you could give the Victory Condition after the first 3 boxes are unlocked, this is like a 5-6 player expansion pack.

- After 45 minutes with 4-6 players engage HARD MODE. Some lights turn red and now the Computer has a time limit on each round. Players need to keep playing it every few minutes otherwise they will GAME OVER and need to leave the room before 1 hour.

- Once the exit is open start a 5 minute countdown to exit. However now the Computer displays continuous Quiz Mode for the questions that were not answered. The players can stay to gain extra points.

### Major Boxes

I still think that MOST of the puzzle components should be findable without locks at the start of the game. The first two Major Boxes should be able to be opened with only things found in the room. The second two need a combination of previously available items and items found in the first Major Boxes. The items in the final two boxes are used to both unlock the exit door (defeat the evil computer) AND for solutions to the Computer Quiz. The game cannot be completed without opening the final two boxes EVEN IF the players know the answers to the Computer Quiz.

### Computer Quiz

The Quiz has three sections. After the players finish one of the Skill Games, the ‘mode’ switches to Quiz. Each Quiz section has a number of questions (6-10) based on different combinations and answers found in that section of the other two Paths.

A random question is displayed and a countdown timer (1-2 minutes?). If the answer is not entered within this time or it is wrong, then a new random question is displayed.

To make this harder, after 45 minutes is up you could add a HARD MODE in which there is only a limited number of lives (wrong answers).

### Computer Skill Games

These are games that much be completed to unlock the next series of Quiz questions. The games should be quite slow paced as the Joystick is on the reverse side to the screen and therefore requires two players.

There should be no clues to real world puzzles in the games as they could be missed.

The games should take about 3-10 minutes. Fastest possible times should be around 3 minutes. No score or powerup items are needed, the games should be simply ‘reach the goal’ type games.

If a game is ‘survive for 5 minutes’ then there must be a failsafe which counts the total time spent in the game. If it takes longer than 15 then it should get easier or just win automatically.

Most of the games on Yodas Videogame Arcade should be good for this.

Left, Right, Up, Down, Shoot. Remember that every verb in the game needs to be described without context.
